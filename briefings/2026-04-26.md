# AI in EdTech Weekly -- 26 April 2026

*A research briefing for the AI Integration Lead, Sydney independent K-12 school. Covering 21--26 April 2026.*

---

## Top Stories

### 1. Microsoft launches Elevate for Educators in Australia alongside A$25 billion infrastructure pledge

**Microsoft | 23 April 2026**

Microsoft announced its largest-ever AI skilling commitment in Australia on 23 April, pledging to train three million Australians in AI skills by 2028 as part of a broader A$25 billion digital infrastructure investment. Accompanying the announcement was the Australian launch of Elevate for Educators -- a free professional learning program providing teachers and school leaders with the AI Literacy for Educators credential, classroom-ready resources, and professional learning communities. Microsoft is also partnering with career guidance platform Anyway (formerly Year13) to give up to 1,000 Australian schools free access to an AI-powered Career Coach for students, and is supporting First Nations AI pathways through Minecraft Education and the Deadly Coders program.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The Elevate for Educators program is immediately actionable for independent schools: it is free, designed for classroom teachers and school leaders, and offers a credential that could anchor a whole-school AI professional learning pathway. The Career Coach offer is worth investigating directly, particularly for schools without a dedicated guidance counsellor. Schools should note, however, that any vendor-designed credential carries implicit curriculum and quality assumptions -- see the Conflict Table below.

Sources: Microsoft News Centre, ["Microsoft announces Australia's largest AI skilling commitment: three million people by 2028"](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/2026/04/23/microsoft-announces-australias-largest-ai-skilling-commitment/), 23 April 2026; Microsoft Source, ["Microsoft deepens commitment to Australia with A$25 billion investment in AI infrastructure, security, and skills"](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/features/investing-in-australias-ai-future/), April 2026.

### 2. eSafety Commissioner urges Australian schools to report deepfakes as incidents double

**eSafety Commissioner | April 2026**

The eSafety Commissioner issued a media release urging schools to report AI-generated deepfake abuse incidents, noting that reports to its office have doubled. The Commissioner also moved against specific services used to "nudify" Australian schoolchildren. A concurrent blog post documents how deepfake technology is actively disrupting students, families, and school communities. The scale is confirmed internationally: a WIRED/Indicator investigation found nearly 90 schools across 28 countries -- including Australia -- with reported incidents of AI-generated nude images of students, affecting at least 600 students; the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (US) reported AI-generated abuse imagery reports rising from 4,700 in 2023 to 440,000 in the first half of 2025 alone.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is no longer a future-risk scenario. Regulatory enforcement has begun, and the eSafety Commissioner is specifically asking schools to report rather than manage incidents internally. Every school needs: a current policy on AI-generated image-based abuse; a clear staff reporting pathway to eSafety; and a student wellbeing response plan that treats victims as such. For primary schools: while most reported incidents involve secondary students, the tools creating this abuse are accessible to primary-age children and are being used by them.

Sources: eSafety Commissioner, ["eSafety urges schools to report deepfakes as numbers double"](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-urges-schools-to-report-deepfakes-as-numbers-double), April 2026; eSafety Commissioner, ["eSafety moves against services used to 'nudify' Australian school children"](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-moves-against-services-used-to-nudify-australian-school-children), April 2026; eSafety Commissioner, ["Deepfake damage in schools"](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/blogs/deepfake-damage-in-schools-how-ai-generated-abuse-is-disrupting-students-families-and-school-communities), January 2026; The Meridiem, ["AI Deepfake Crisis Crosses 90 Schools as Consumer Tools Enable Abuse at Scale"](https://themeridiem.com/ai-machine-learning/2026/04/15/ai-deepfake-crisis-crosses-90-schools-as-consumer-tools-enable-abuse-at-scale), 15 April 2026.

### 3. Australian researchers warn that unstructured AI use in schools risks cognitive atrophy

**University of Technology Sydney / Australian Network for Quality Digital Education | March 2026**

A report by Professor Leslie Loble AM (UTS) and Professor Jason Lodge (University of Queensland) for the Australian Network for Quality Digital Education argues that while AI can deepen learning, unstructured use risks students developing "surface-level mastery" as cognitive effort is offloaded to the machine. The paper, "Artificial Intelligence, Cognitive Offloading and Implications for Education," frames the central challenge as designing AI use that builds students' self-regulated learning and critical thinking rather than substituting for it. Equity is a central concern: students without strong foundational skills are most susceptible to counterproductive offloading, meaning poorly designed AI use is likely to widen existing learning gaps.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the most practically grounded Australian paper in the recent literature. The productive/counterproductive offloading distinction gives classroom teachers a usable heuristic: is this AI task asking students to practise higher-order thinking, or replacing it? The report calls for national standards for AI tools in schools and for investment in teacher capability -- both policy asks directly relevant to independent school advocacy and procurement decisions.

Sources: UTS News, ["Experts warn unstructured AI use in schools risks 'cognitive atrophy'"](https://www.uts.edu.au/news/2026/03/experts-warn-unstructured-ai-use-in-schools-risks-cognitive-atrophy), March 2026; EducationHQ, ["'Surface-level mastery': cognitive atrophy a risk of schools' unchecked AI use"](https://educationhq.com/news/surface-level-mastery-cognitive-atrophy-a-risk-of-schools-unchecked-ai-use-207204/), March 2026; full report PDF available at [UTS](https://www.uts.edu.au/news/2026/03/experts-warn-unstructured-ai-use-in-schools-risks-cognitive-atrophy/contentassets/ai-cognitive-offloading-and-implications-for-education.pdf).

### 4. US marks one year of its AI-in-education executive order; Department of Education issues new funding guidance

**White House / US Department of Education | 23--24 April 2026**

The White House Office of Science and Technology Policy marked the one-year anniversary of President Trump's "Advancing Artificial Intelligence Education for American Youth" executive order on 24 April, reporting implementation milestones: expanded K-12 educator AI training nationally, federal grant programs realigned to AI education priorities, and more than 200 companies and nonprofits pledging free AI education resources for students. The following day, the US Department of Education issued guidance on how grantees may use federal education funds for AI-related purposes, explicitly listing personalised learning, educator professional development, AI literacy, and tools to detect AI-generated disinformation as supported uses.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the most significant international policy signal of the week. Australia has a national AI framework but not a comparably funded federal mandate for AI in schools. The US model -- one year old, now with documented resourcing and a progress tracker at AI.gov -- offers an increasingly concrete comparison point as Australian education ministers consider whether voluntary frameworks are sufficient. The explicit funding of tools to detect AI-generated disinformation is particularly notable; this capability is not yet a named priority in any Australian state or federal AI education framework.

Sources: Life News Agency, ["White House Marks One-Year Anniversary of Trump Executive Order on AI Education for America's Youth"](https://lifenewsagency.com/white-house-marks-one-year-anniversary-of-trump-executive-order-on-ai-education-for-americas-youth/), 24 April 2026; US Department of Education, ["US Department of Education Issues Guidance on Artificial Intelligence Use in Schools"](https://www.ed.gov/about/news/press-release/us-department-of-education-issues-guidance-artificial-intelligence-use-schools-proposes-additional-supplemental-priority), April 2026.

### 5. OpenAI publishes early evidence that Study Mode improves exam scores -- and commits to longitudinal research with 20,000 students

**OpenAI | April 2026**

OpenAI published results from a study of more than 300 university students using ChatGPT's Study Mode, finding a 15% higher exam score in microeconomics compared to a control group. More significant than the result is the research infrastructure being built: in partnership with Estonia's University of Tartu and Stanford's SCALE Initiative, OpenAI is conducting a longitudinal study with nearly 20,000 students aged 16-18 to measure whether AI-assisted learning gains are durable on unassisted assessments -- a question the existing evidence base has not been able to answer.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The 15% headline will travel quickly through vendor marketing. The more important signal is the design of Study Mode itself: it scaffolds student thinking with hints and questions rather than delivering answers. This aligns with the pedagogical guardrails that Stanford's broader K-12 evidence review identifies as the distinguishing feature of AI tools that actually improve learning. The longitudinal study should produce upper-secondary-relevant data within 12-18 months; it is worth tracking.

Source: OpenAI, ["New tools for understanding AI and learning outcomes"](https://openai.com/index/understanding-ai-and-learning-outcomes/), April 2026.

## Worth Watching

**1. LAK26 Conference, Bergen, Norway (27 April -- 1 May 2026)**

The Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference begins this week, presenting peer-reviewed research on AI and learning analytics in K-12 and higher education. This is one of the highest-quality venues for causal research on AI in classrooms. Several papers flagged in last week's briefing will be presented, including the NC State "sticky teacher help" study. Monitor the conference program for findings relevant to primary classrooms and AI tutoring equity.

**2. OpenAI Education for Countries initiative**

OpenAI has launched a proposal to build national AI learning stacks in partnership with governments, offering tools, infrastructure, and curriculum resources at a systemic level. No Australian government engagement has been announced, but the pitch is active in multiple countries. If any Australian state or federal body enters a formal arrangement with OpenAI for curriculum or assessment infrastructure, the governance, data sovereignty, and vendor-dependency implications for independent schools would be substantial -- and worth raising before any such arrangement is finalised.
