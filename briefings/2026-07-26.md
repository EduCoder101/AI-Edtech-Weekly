# AI in EdTech Weekly -- 26 July 2026

*A research briefing for the AI Integration Lead, Sydney independent K-12 school. Covering 20 July -- 26 July 2026.*

## Top Stories

One Australian story cleared the significance threshold; the remainder below are international items with clear Australian relevance, flagged rather than padded with lesser local items.*

### 1. AI companions come under the regulatory microscope, and Australian researchers ask what schools should learn

**The Conversation | 23 July 2026**

Australian academics Joseph Crawford (University of Tasmania) and Michael Cowling published an analysis this week of China's new AI companion law, which took effect on 15 July and forced major companion services including features of Doubao and Qwen to shut down or restrict emotionally responsive chat, with millions of users losing conversation histories. The authors examine what Australia, which has led internationally on youth social media restrictions, should take from China's harder line. They warn of an AI-induced "loneliness spiral": lonely and socially anxious young people turn to conversational AI for relief, which can displace human friendships and deepen the original problem. In the same week, the eSafety Commissioner ran a professional learning webinar for educators (23 July) covering AI companion chatbots, sexual and non-sexual deepfakes, AI influencers, and AI-facilitated cyberbullying.

**Why it matters for K-12:** AI companions are the fastest-moving child-safety issue in the AI space, and this week shows both ends of the response spectrum: China has legislated bluntly, while Australia is so far working through eSafety enforcement notices and educator capability. For a school, the practical takeaway is that companion use is a wellbeing issue, not a technology issue: it belongs in pastoral care and parent communication, not just the acceptable use policy. The eSafety educator webinar programme is a low-cost way to build staff awareness before an incident forces the conversation.

Sources: [China has cracked down on AI companions. What can we learn from this?](https://theconversation.com/china-has-cracked-down-on-ai-companions-what-can-we-learn-from-this-287976) -- The Conversation, 23 July 2026; [China AI Companion Law Takes Effect](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320525/20260715/china-ai-companion-law-takes-effect-doubao-qwen-shut-down-millions-lose-chat-data.htm) -- Tech Times, 15 July 2026; [Professional learning for educators](https://www.esafety.gov.au/educators/training-for-professionals/educators-and-youth-serving-professionals) -- eSafety Commissioner, webinar 23 July 2026.

### 2. Instructure's 2026 State of AI in Education survey: parents, teachers and students now share the same three worries

**Instructure / K-12 Dive | 21 July 2026**

Instructure released its State of AI in Education 2026 survey at InstructureCon on 21 July, drawing on 1,125 US respondents across K-12 educators, higher education students and K-12 parents. Use is now mainstream: 68% of K-12 educators and 73% of parents report using AI or seeing their children use it at least occasionally. Strikingly, all three groups converge on the same top concerns: overreliance on AI, loss of critical thinking and deep learning, and academic integrity. Around 60-69% across groups worry that AI "sounds confident even when wrong". All groups accept AI more readily for support tasks (explaining concepts, brainstorming, finding resources) than for grading or academic decisions. The training gap remains stark: 45% of K-12 educators report no formal AI training, and only 8% describe their training as comprehensive.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The alignment between parents and teachers is the useful finding. A school proposing clear guardrails (AI for support tasks, never for assessment decisions, always with teacher oversight) is now pushing on an open door with its parent community, and the survey gives concrete language for that conversation. The training figures also give benchmarking ammunition for a professional learning budget: if under one in ten teachers anywhere describe their AI training as comprehensive, a school that invests properly here differentiates itself.

Sources: [AI in education research: what the data shows](https://www.instructure.com/research/ai-2.0) -- Instructure, 21 July 2026; [AI embraced by more students and educators, Instructure finds](https://www.k12dive.com/news/ai-embraced-by-more-students-and-educators-instructure-finds/825826/) -- K-12 Dive, July 2026; [Survey: Gap exists between students, educators when it comes to using, understanding AI](https://www.deseret.com/utah/2026/07/22/survey-gap-exists-between-students-educators-when-it-comes-to-using-ai/) -- Deseret News, 22 July 2026.

### 3. OpenAI consolidates teen safety controls and lets parents make Study Mode the default

**OpenAI | 13-19 July 2026**

Just ahead of this coverage window, and not covered in last week's issue, OpenAI rolled out a consolidated set of teen-focused changes under a policy post titled "Why teens deserve access to safe AI" (16 July). Parents with linked teen accounts can now make Study Mode, the Socratic tutoring feature that guides rather than answers, the default for every new chat their teen starts. Parental safety alerts were widened on 13 July to include accounts deactivated for violent threats, and teens now get more frequent break reminders and education-focused starter prompts. Coverage and analysis of the changes continued through this week. The launch drew immediate criticism from advocacy group Schools Beyond Screens, which called on 16 July for a moratorium on default-on AI tools on school-issued devices.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the clearest signal yet that the major AI platforms expect regulated, parent-mediated teen access to become the norm, rather than age bans. For schools, the parent-controlled Study Mode default is worth knowing about in detail: it is the first mainstream mechanism that lets families enforce a pedagogically defensible mode of AI use at home, and parents will ask about it. It also complicates the school's messaging if school guidance treats ChatGPT as a single undifferentiated tool when home use may already be locked to a tutoring mode.

Sources: [OpenAI Expands Teen Safety Features and Study Mode in ChatGPT](https://www.tun.com/home/openai-expands-teen-safety-features-and-study-mode-in-chatgpt/) -- The University Network, July 2026; [ChatGPT Introduces Teen Safety Features with Break Reminders, Study Mode, and Quiet Hours](https://www.itechpost.com/articles/236724/20260717/chatgpt-introduces-teen-safety-features-break-reminders-study-mode-quiet-hours.htm) -- iTech Post, 17 July 2026; [OpenAI Parent Alerts Expand to Teen ChatGPT Violence Bans](https://techmymoney.com/2026/07/19/openai-parent-alerts-expand-to-teen-chatgpt-violence-bans/) -- TechMyMoney, 19 July 2026.

### 4. Teacher AI capability moves up the global policy agenda: Egypt launches a national competency framework, UNESCO opens a global ethics MOOC

**UNESCO | 20-21 July 2026**

Two UNESCO-linked developments this week point the same way. Egypt launched a national AI competency framework for teachers on 21 July, one of the first countries to translate UNESCO's 2024 AI competency framework for teachers into a binding national structure. A day earlier, UNESCO and LG AI Research launched a free global MOOC on the ethics of AI (20 July). These sit alongside the ongoing UNESCO-EU FutureProof Education project, which this month moved into a phase of turning teacher co-design workshops into practical toolkits for schools across six European systems.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Australia has a national framework for generative AI in schools but no national teacher AI competency framework; AITSL guidance remains advisory. When countries as different as Egypt, Ireland and North Rhine-Westphalia converge on formal teacher AI competency structures, it becomes more likely that Australian regulators follow, plausibly through AITSL standards or the promised national AI standards. A school that maps its professional learning against the UNESCO teacher competency framework now will find any future compliance exercise largely done.

Sources: [Artificial intelligence in education](https://www.unesco.org/en/digital-education/artificial-intelligence) -- UNESCO (Egypt framework, 21 July 2026; ethics MOOC, 20 July 2026); [UNESCO-EU school AI project moves to teacher-led toolkit phase](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/unesco-eu-school-ai-project-moves-to-teacher-led-toolkit-phase) -- EdTech Innovation Hub, July 2026; [UNESCO AI competency framework for teachers](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/what-you-need-know-about-unescos-new-ai-competency-frameworks-students-and-teachers) -- UNESCO.

## Worth Watching

**1. Queensland's Artificial Intelligence in Education Conference, Cairns, 29 July**

Queensland's education department holds its second AI in Education conference in Cairns on 29 July, following the statewide rollout of Corella, its government-owned AI assistant for teachers and students. Watch for implementation lessons from the first cohort of Corella schools: Queensland is running the largest state-built classroom AI deployment outside NSW, and what surfaces here tends to reach the independent sector's conversation a term later.

Source: [Artificial Intelligence in Education Conference, Queensland](https://www.informa.com.au/event/conference/education/artificial-intelligence-in-education-conference-queensland/) -- Informa/Queensland Department of Education, 29 July 2026.

**2. National Cabinet's August meeting on draft national AI standards**

Carried over from last week and now imminent: the draft framework of legislated national AI standards, explicitly covering education and training, goes to National Cabinet in August. No new detail emerged this week, which itself is worth noting; schools should expect the substance to land with little additional warning. The open questions remain whether independent schools are named alongside government schools and whether teacher and student AI literacy appear as obligations.

Source: [AI in Australia's Interests](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/ai-australias-interests) -- Prime Minister of Australia, 15 July 2026.
