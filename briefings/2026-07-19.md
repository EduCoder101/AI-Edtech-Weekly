# AI in EdTech Weekly -- 19 July 2026

*A research briefing for the AI Integration Lead, Sydney independent K-12 school. Covering 12 July -- 19 July 2026.*

---

## Top Stories

### 1. Albanese announces Office of AI and national standards covering education and training

**Prime Minister of Australia | 15 July 2026**

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced on 15 July 2026 that Australia will establish an Office of Artificial Intelligence within the Department of Prime Minister and Cabinet. The Office will coordinate a set of legislated national standards for AI, to be presented at National Cabinet in August 2026 with legislation intended for early 2027. The standards are explicitly framed to cover education and training, workplace rights and productivity, data centre energy obligations, and copyright protections for creators. Albanese framed the announcement as putting "Australian values as the benchmark" for how AI operates in this country, and rejected calls from industry to loosen copyright law to allow AI models to be trained on Australian content without creator consent.

**Why it matters for K-12:** If national AI standards include enforceable obligations for the education and training sector -- which the announcement strongly implies -- independent schools will need to ensure their AI policies, procurement decisions, and student-facing tool deployments align with standards that carry legal weight, not merely with the existing voluntary framework. The August National Cabinet meeting is the next significant date to watch. It is also worth noting that the government's explicit rejection of an AI copyright carve-out has implications for any school using AI tools trained on potentially unlicensed content.

### 2. Education Ministers meet in Parramatta on AI in schools, Jason Clare calls for national deepfake standards

**Ministers' Media Centre | 15 July 2026**

Concurrent with the Office of AI announcement, Education Minister Jason Clare met with state and territory education ministers in Parramatta on 15 July to discuss the direct impacts of AI on school communities. The agenda centred on deepfakes: Clare called explicitly for national standards to combat AI-generated non-consensual intimate images (NCIIs) of students, which he described as "the latest frontier of bullying." The meeting noted that female students and, increasingly, female teachers are being targeted. The discussions also addressed AI-harvested data breaches in schools and the presence of AI-generated child sexual abuse material (CSAM) in school communities. The ministerial communique follows the eSafety Commissioner's earlier action requiring Character AI, Nomi, Chai, and Chub AI to explain how they are keeping children safe.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is no longer a peripheral concern -- it is on the agenda of a dedicated national ministerial meeting. For independent schools in NSW, the combination of a federal push for national deepfake standards and existing state laws on image-based abuse means the policy environment is tightening rapidly. Your school's response plan needs to cover three distinct but related issues: student-on-student targeting, targeting of staff (especially female teachers), and CSAM involving school-connected imagery. The eSafety Commissioner's educator resources on deepfakes and AI companions are the most practical starting point.

Sources: [Education Minister Jason Clare calls for States to crack down on deepfakes nudifying students](https://thenightly.com.au/politics/education-minister-jason-clare-calls-for-states-to-crack-down-on-deepfakes-nudifying-students-c-19171617) -- The Nightly, July 2026; [New AI agency in Prime Minister's department to guide controversial technology's use](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9310618/anthony-albanese-established-new-pmc-ai-office-to-make-australia-world-first/) -- Canberra Times, July 2026; [eSafety requires providers of AI companion chatbots to explain how they are keeping Aussie kids safe](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-requires-providers-of-ai-companion-chatbots-to-explain-how-they-are-keeping-aussie-kids-safe) -- eSafety Commissioner, 2026.

### 3. More than half of Australian university assignments now contain AI content, Turnitin data shows

**The Conversation | July 2026**

A widely circulated Conversation article this week draws on data from Turnitin showing that 53.6% of Australian tertiary education submissions processed through its system from October 2025 to April 2026 contained some AI-generated content. Ten per cent of those submissions were more than 80% AI-written. The article -- which the data itself would not have permitted a year ago -- is notable because it uses the figure not as an indictment of students but as a prompt to ask what universities should do differently. Separate data from Anthropic cited in coverage this week showed Australia now leads in per capita use of its Claude chatbot, with coursework making up a significant share of that use. Curtin University has disabled the AI detection feature in Turnitin from 1 January 2026, citing reliability concerns.

**Why it matters for K-12:** University trajectories routinely arrive in secondary schools within two to three years. If more than half of assessed work submitted to Australian universities now includes AI content, Year 11 and 12 teachers and school leaders need to be designing now for what assessment integrity looks like when that pattern reaches HSC-facing cohorts in greater numbers. The Curtin decision to disable AI detection is also worth noting: if detection tools are becoming unreliable at the tertiary level, the same tools in secondary school contexts carry the same risks of false accusations, particularly for students who are non-native English speakers.

Sources: [More than 50% of Australian university assignments used AI. How should unis respond?](https://theconversation.com/more-than-50-of-australian-university-assignments-used-ai-how-should-unis-respond-287179) -- The Conversation, July 2026; [Turnitin Q2 Integrity Insights Report](https://www.turnitin.com/press/us-higher-education-students-outsourcing-writing-to-ai-at-twice-the-rate-of-uk-australian-peers) -- Turnitin, 2026; [Curtin University to disable Turnitin AI detection tool in 2026](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/curtin-university-to-disable-turnitin-ai-detection-tool-in-2026-as-debate-over-reliability-continues) -- EdTech Innovation Hub, 2026.

### 4. Anthropic launches Claude for Teachers, entering the race to become the classroom AI of choice

**Chalkbeat | 14 July 2026**

Anthropic launched Claude for Teachers on 14 July 2026, offering free access to verified K-12 educators in the United States until June 2027. The product joins Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, and OpenAI's ChatGPT Edu as the major platforms competing for adoption in schools. Key features include lesson planning, personalised instructional materials, and data-informed differentiation. The launch includes nine "education connectors" -- pre-built integrations with ASSISTments, Brisk Teaching, Canva Education, Coteach, Diffit, Eedi, MagicSchool, Snorkl, and TeachFX. Anthropic has committed that conversations on verified teacher accounts will not be used to train its models, and that student information is covered by a school data processing agreement intended to comply with FERPA.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The product is currently US-only and free, but the pattern matters more than the specific offering. Every major AI platform now has an education-specific product, and all are competing for the habitual use of teachers before school procurement cycles formalise. The connector ecosystem is particularly relevant: Canva Education and MagicSchool are already in use in many Australian schools. When teachers begin using a platform casually through these connectors, the school's data governance position can become unclear. This is a good moment to check whether your school's AI policy addresses platform-specific data agreements, not just AI use in general.

Sources: [Anthropic launches Claude for Teachers in AI race to influence America's classrooms](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2026/07/14/anthropic-launches-claude-for-teachers-as-ai-companies-battle-for-classrooms/) -- Chalkbeat, 14 July 2026; [Anthropic launches free Claude for Teachers](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/5968601-claude-for-teachers-launch/) -- The Hill, 14 July 2026; [Anthropic Launches AI For Teachers](https://www.forbes.com/sites/danfitzpatrick/2026/07/14/anthropic-launches-ai-for-teachers/) -- Forbes, 14 July 2026.

### 5. Day of AI Australia's Google.org partnership yields new student AI ethics games

**Day of AI Australia | 17 July 2026**

Day of AI Australia -- an initiative developed in partnership with UNSW Sydney and adapted locally from MIT RAISE's global program -- held its 2026 event on 17 July. This week's event builds on an earlier announcement that the Day of AI-UNSW partnership was selected as the only Australian recipient of Google.org's Generative AI Accelerator, which distributes a share of $30 million and pro bono technical support to organisations using generative AI to address societal challenges. The partnership is developing new curriculum-aligned experiences including "Win the Farm," an immersive game where students configure AI-powered bots to generate social media posts in a fictional election, directly engaging with algorithmic influence and democratic discourse. An AI ethics game and modules on data privacy and the natural world are also in development.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is one of the most practically useful Australian AI literacy initiatives for primary and secondary classrooms. The materials are being built specifically for Australian students, are free, and are grounded in the kind of critical AI literacy -- rather than tool use -- that the Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools calls for. If your school does not already use Day of AI modules, this is a good point to evaluate them. The Google.org backing also means the program is likely to expand in scope and reach over the next two years.

Sources: [AI education boost: Partnership to build GenAI experiences for schools](https://dayofaiaustralia.com/ai-education-boost-partnership-to-build-genai-experiences-for-schools/) -- Day of AI Australia; [July 17, 2026 event](https://dayofaiaustralia.com/2026/07/17/) -- Day of AI Australia; [UNSW Day of AI Australia case study](https://cloud.google.com/customers/unswdayofaiaustralia) -- Google Cloud.

## Worth Watching

**1. National Cabinet AI standards meeting, August 2026**

The Office of AI announcement named August's National Cabinet meeting as the next significant milestone, at which a draft framework of national AI standards will be presented for discussion. If education is embedded in those standards -- as Albanese's speech strongly implies -- this would create the first binding, cross-jurisdictional obligations on schools around AI use, data governance, and student safety. Watch for: whether independent schools are included alongside government schools, whether there is an implementation timeline, and whether student AI literacy features as an obligation or just an aspiration.

Source: [AI in Australia's Interests](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/ai-australias-interests) -- Prime Minister of Australia, 15 July 2026.

**2. UNICEF: children are adopting AI at more than three times the adult rate**

A June 2026 UNICEF Innocenti brief found that in 10 surveyed countries, up to 50% of children are already using AI tools, including one in ten who turn to AI for advice about things that worry them. A separate UNICEF policy brief on AI companions documents how children in multiple jurisdictions are forming emotional attachments to AI chatbots in ways that raise safeguarding and developmental concerns, and compares regulatory responses across six jurisdictions. Neither brief is Australia-specific, but both describe dynamics that are visible in Australian schools. The companion risks in particular connect directly to the eSafety Commissioner's ongoing enforcement actions. The combination of high adoption rates and emotional reliance on AI companions is worth tracking for its implications for wellbeing programs and parent communication.

Sources: [Snapshot of AI Usage and Concerns Among Children and Parents](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/documents/snapshot-ai-usage-among-children-parents) -- UNICEF Innocenti, June 2026; [When AI becomes a friend: Child rights risks, harms, and regulatory responses to AI chatbots and companions](https://www.unicef.org/documents/when-ai-becomes-friend-child-rights-risks) -- UNICEF, June 2026.
