# AI in EdTech Weekly -- 4 October 2026

*A research briefing for the AI Integration Lead, Sydney independent K-12 school. Covering 28 September--4 October 2026.*

---

## Top Stories

### 1. The SA Royal Commission into AI is underway, and education is first on its agenda

**ABC News / Capital Brief / InDaily | 1 October 2026**

South Australia's Royal Commission into Artificial Intelligence, the first of its kind in the world, formally commenced on 1 October with the announcement of its three commissioners: former Federal Court judge and Fair Work Commission president Dr Iain Ross AO as chair, former Tech Council of Australia chief executive (and former OpenAI policy liaison) Kate Pounder, and Stanford University machine learning expert Professor Christopher Manning. The terms of reference direct the commission to propose policy, legislative or structural reform on AI's implications across education, employment, economic opportunity, regulation, community life and public services, with a final report due by 1 July 2027.

The detail that matters most for schools: education, skills and training are expected to be the subject of the commission's first issues paper, and the commission will take public submissions alongside community forums, surveys and targeted stakeholder consultations. The $3 million inquiry has a compressed nine-month timeline, which means the education consultation window will open early and close fast.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Previous briefings flagged this as the moment to have evidence ready, and the commission has now confirmed education goes first. Whatever the commission recommends will shape how every Australian jurisdiction, not just SA, regulates AI in schools, and a well-evidenced submission from an independent school actually doing the integration work would carry weight precisely because so little Australian classroom evidence exists (the University of Sydney and Barker Institute review found four Australian studies out of 271 worldwide). The practical move is to finalise the school's documented position now and coordinate with ISA and AISNSW on a sector submission before the issues paper lands.

### 2. Australian schools are scaling up edtech, and 60 per cent want education-specific AI

**Campion Education / Educate Ventures Research | reported 28 September 2026**

New national research from Campion Education and Educate Ventures Research, based on a survey of 126 Australian school respondents plus in-depth interviews with five school leaders, found 56 per cent of schools intend to scale up their use of education technology over the next twelve months, and 60 per cent are extremely interested in education-specific generative AI. General-purpose generative AI has already settled into routine teacher practice: 55 per cent of respondents use it for lesson preparation and 45 per cent for planning, while dedicated teaching and learning tools are most used for student practice (50 per cent) and classroom teaching (46 per cent).

The qualitative findings are as useful as the numbers. Schools said successful technology must align with learning goals, simplify rather than add complexity, be sustainable over time and come from trusted providers, a procurement sensibility that has clearly hardened since the first wave of AI enthusiasm.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The 60 per cent appetite for education-specific AI, against a backdrop of teachers already using general-purpose tools for planning and preparation, maps the gap the next procurement cycle will try to fill, and it is exactly the space vendors are now racing into. The four criteria schools named (aligned, simple, sustainable, trusted) are a ready-made evaluation rubric, and they echo the AFT agreement terms from a fortnight ago. Worth noting the sample is modest at 126 respondents, so treat the percentages as directional rather than definitive.

### 3. Google opens the Gemini student hub to school students of all ages, inside the platform most schools already run

**Google Workspace Updates / EdTech Magazine | 1-2 October 2026**

Google announced this week that its Gemini student hub, a dedicated space in the Gemini app bundling study notebooks, flashcard generation, practice quizzes and study-planning tools, is now available to Google Workspace for Education users of all ages, not just over-18s. The hub sits behind the existing generative AI settings in the Workspace Admin console, which means whether students encounter it is determined by each school's tenancy configuration, and it is reaching domains now through Google's rapid and scheduled release tracks.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Most Australian independent schools run on Workspace for Education, so this is not a tool students need to seek out: it arrives inside the account they already use for schoolwork. The decision in front of every school this term is a deliberate configuration call, which year levels see Gemini and its study tools, made with teaching and wellbeing staff rather than left to a default. There is also a pedagogical distinction worth teaching: tools that scaffold retrieval practice from a student's own class materials are architecturally different from an open chatbot that completes tasks, and the difference between the two is precisely the AI-literacy line PISA's data rewards. This is the pattern last week's Education Week reporting predicted: the meaningful control point is tenancy configuration, not policy headlines.

Sources: [Find your learning tools all in one place with the student hub in Gemini](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/find-your-learning-tools-all-in-one-place-with-the-student-hub-in-Gemini.html) -- Google Workspace Updates, October 2026; [Google Workspace Weekly Recap - October 2, 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/weekly-recap-10-02-2026.html) -- Google Workspace Updates, 2 October 2026; [Google Opens Gemini AI Study Tools to Younger Students](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/10/01/google-opens-classrooms-gemini-study-tools-to-younger-students-xcxwbn/) -- Winbuzzer, 1 October 2026; [Google Launches New AI Tools Purpose-Built for Education](https://edtechmagazine.com/k12/article/2026/10/google-launches-new-ai-tools-purpose-built-education) -- EdTech Magazine, October 2026.

### 4. Cambridge report: give teachers a formal voice in AI policy, and fund the professional learning to back it

**Cambridge University Press & Assessment | 1 October 2026**

A new Cambridge University Press & Assessment report, Shaping AI Policy and Practice in English Language Teaching, distils the practice of early-adopter teachers worldwide into eight principles for AI integration, and its headline recommendation is aimed at policymakers: teachers and learners should contribute directly to decisions about how generative AI tools are implemented and evaluated, so policy is grounded in classroom realities rather than imposed on them. The principles include investing in sustained (not one-off) teacher professional learning, embedding critical AI literacy as a curriculum expectation so students learn to question AI output and know when not to use it, reforming assessment to recognise process evidence and learner reflection alongside final products, prioritising human capabilities such as critical thinking and autonomy, and protecting teacher agency in tool selection and classroom integration.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Though framed around English language teaching, the eight principles read as a general-purpose blueprint and align closely with where Australian policy has landed this term: NESA's shift from detection to assessment design, and PISA's finding that structured critique of AI output lifts outcomes while task-substitution depresses them. For a school building its 2027 professional learning programme, the report's insistence on sustained PL over one-off sessions, and on teachers as decision-makers rather than recipients, is a credible external anchor to cite. It also lands just as the SA Royal Commission's education issues paper process begins, and its "teacher voice in governance" argument belongs in any submission.

Sources: [Policymakers must give teachers a stronger voice on AI policy in schools, new Cambridge report recommends](https://www.fenews.co.uk/education/policymakers-must-give-teachers-a-stronger-voice-on-ai-policy-in-schools-new-cambridge-report-recommends/) -- FE News, 1 October 2026; [New Cambridge report on AI integration in schools](https://www.elgazette.com/new-cambridge-report-on-ai-integration-in-schools/) -- EL Gazette, October 2026; [Policymakers in Canada must give teachers a stronger voice on AI policy in schools](https://educationnewscanada.com/article/education/level/university/1/1220593/policymakers-in-canada-must-give-teachers-a-stronger-voice-on-ai-policy-in-schools-new-cambridge-report-recommends.html) -- Education News Canada / University of Cambridge, October 2026.

### 5. California closes its legislative year with a dozen AI laws: student data off limits for training, human teachers required, and a landmark kids' AI bill vetoed

**EdSource / CalMatters / Transparency Coalition | 1-2 October 2026**

Governor Gavin Newsom ended California's 2026 bill-signing period on 1 October having enacted around a dozen new AI measures, capping a year of nearly thirty. The education-relevant core: AB 1159 prohibits technology companies from using K-12 student data to train or develop AI models; SB 928 specifies that California State University instructors must be human, not AI; and the chatbot child-safety package signed earlier in September (including Adam's Law, SB 1119) now requires crisis protocols, expanded parental controls and annual independent child-safety audits for AI companion chatbots used by minors. Newsom also vetoed AB 1064, the LEAD for Kids Act, which would have regulated any generative AI system made for children, arguing its language was overly broad; he signed the narrower, data-focused AB 1159 instead. Tracking by the Transparency Coalition counts 78 chatbot bills alive across 27 US states.

**Why it matters for K-12:** California tends to set the de facto national standard for US edtech vendors, and AB 1159's no-training-on-student-data rule now sits alongside the AFT-Microsoft agreement as the second major instrument forcing that term into vendor practice. Products sold into Australian schools will increasingly be built to these rules by default, which strengthens an Australian school's hand in demanding the same contractual terms. The AB 1064 veto is the other lesson: even California balked at regulating "AI for children" as a category, opting for specific, enforceable rules about data and chatbots, the same granular turn visible in NESA's approach.

## Worth Watching

**1. Two federal submission windows: the AI standards consultation closes Friday, and the Joint Select Committee reports in November**

The Office of AI's consultation on Australia's first nationally consistent AI standards ("Getting it right: Building AI infrastructure that works for Australia") closes on 9 October. Its current focus is data centres and model training rather than school obligations, but it includes sectoral impact questions and will shape the regulation that eventually governs school-facing tools. Separately, the federal Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence, whose terms include the adequacy of the education system for an AI-transformed workforce, is working towards its 30 November reporting deadline. With the SA Royal Commission also opening its process, three submission channels are live at once; ISA and AISNSW remain the efficient route for a school-level contribution.

Sources: [Getting it right: Building AI infrastructure that works for Australia](https://www.pmc.gov.au/resources/getting-it-right-building-ai-infrastructure-works-australia) -- Office of AI, Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, September 2026; [From patchwork to playbook: the Joint Select Committee on AI and what it means for business](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2026/august/from-patchwork-to-playbook-the-joint-select-committee-on-ai-and-what-it-means-for-business) -- Clayton Utz, August 2026.

**2. Persistent AI agents are coming to the tools students and staff already use**

At its DevDay on 29 September, OpenAI introduced "Dots", persistent agents that keep working towards a user's goals across sessions and connect to thousands of apps. Agentic AI that acts over time, rather than answering single prompts, raises a different set of school questions: what an agent remembers about a student, what it can do in a linked Google or Microsoft account, and whether teen-mode protections carry across agent features. Nothing to act on yet, but the next tenancy configuration review should ask whether agent features are reaching school accounts.

Sources: [AI News of the Week, 2 October 2026](https://manaknightdigital.com/blog/ai-news-week-of-october-02-2026) -- Manaknight Digital, 2 October 2026; [AI Update, October 02, 2026](https://marketingprofs.com/opinions/2026/56056/ai-update-october-02-2026-ai-news-and-views-from-the-past-week) -- MarketingProfs, 2 October 2026.
