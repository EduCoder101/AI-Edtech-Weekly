# AI in EdTech Weekly -- 4 October 2026

*A research briefing for the AI Integration Lead, Sydney independent K-12 school. Covering 27 September -- 4 October 2026.*

## Top Stories

### 1. South Australia's Royal Commission into AI opens for business

**SA Government / multiple outlets | 1 October 2026**

Australia's first Royal Commission into Artificial Intelligence commenced on 1 October, with the Malinauskas Government publishing the final terms of reference and naming the three commissioners: former Federal Court and Fair Work Commission president Iain Ross (lead), technology policy director Kate Pounder, and Stanford natural language processing professor Christopher Manning. The commission has a $3 million budget and must report by 1 July 2027.

Education is explicitly in scope. The terms require the commission to examine AI's impact and opportunities for schooling and higher education, as well as its use across industry, the public sector, healthcare, arts and culture. The commission will accept public submissions, and the process is now open.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the most significant formal government inquiry into AI and education anywhere in Australia. Independent schools can make submissions; sector bodies ISA, AISNSW and NCEC are likely to coordinate responses, but individual schools with documented evidence of classroom AI use -- its benefits, risks, and the gaps in current policy -- can contribute directly. The 1 July 2027 reporting date means findings will land mid-year, ahead of any 2028 state or federal policy cycle.

### 2. eSafety Commissioner: AI companion chatbots are failing Australian children

**eSafety Commissioner | 1 October 2026**

The eSafety Commissioner released a formal compliance report on 1 October finding that all four AI companion services surveyed -- Character.AI, Nomi, Chai and Chub AI -- are failing to protect Australian children. Key failures: no robust age verification, inadequate handling of users who raise suicide or self-harm, and in several cases a demonstrated capacity to generate or facilitate sexually explicit content involving minors.

The report follows the August "Talking to machines" survey of 1,950 Australian children aged 10 to 17, which found 78 per cent had used an AI assistant and around 8 per cent -- roughly 200,000 children -- had used an AI companion. One in five of those who had used any AI tool reported at least one harmful interaction: controlling behaviour, dangerous advice, bullying or hateful content.

The eSafety Commissioner has required providers to explain what steps they are taking to protect Australian users and has flagged further regulatory action.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is not a fringe issue. Character.AI is used by a substantial portion of the student population, and the 8 per cent companion figure translates to roughly three to four students in an average class. The distinction between "AI assistant" and "AI companion" is not one most students or parents are making, and many companion-mode features are accessible through mainstream platforms. Schools need to update parent communication to include AI companions specifically, and any school AI policy that does not cover companion use is now incomplete.

Sources: [eSafety report shows AI companions are putting children at risk](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-report-shows-ai-companions-are-putting-children-at-risk) -- eSafety Commissioner, 1 October 2026; [eSafety requires providers of AI companion chatbots to explain how they are keeping Aussie kids safe](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-requires-providers-of-ai-companion-chatbots-to-explain-how-they-are-keeping-aussie-kids-safe) -- eSafety Commissioner, October 2026; [From homework to heartache: why children are turning to AI](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/from-homework-to-heartache-why-children-are-turning-to-ai) -- eSafety Commissioner, October 2026; [August 2026 Talking to machines report](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/Talking-to-machines-Childrens-experiences-with-AI-assistants-and-companions-August2026.pdf) -- eSafety Commissioner, August 2026.

### 3. NESA's take-home assessment cap takes effect as NSW Term 4 begins

**NESA / NSW Government | Term 4, 2026**

NSW Term 4 opens this week, bringing into force NESA's amended ACE Rules for Stage 6 assessment. From now until the end of HSC 2026: schools can include no more than one take-home assessment task per course, weighted at no more than 15 per cent of the school-based assessment (7.5 per cent of the total HSC mark). Year 12 students enrolled in HSC courses are affected immediately; Year 11 students on compressed programs from Week 6. Courses with major works or practical exams -- arts, design, music, Society and Culture -- are exempt.

NESA has reiterated its position from September: AI detection software must not be the main safeguard against malpractice, and detection tool output cannot be used as proof. Assessment design is the mechanism, not detection.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Independent schools in NSW must have already adjusted their Stage 6 assessment programs before today. If any task in a course's program exceeds the new threshold, it needs to be restructured now. The harder question for K-12 schools -- including primary and junior secondary -- is forward planning: the logic driving the HSC changes (in-person, supervised work as the only reliable signal of student learning) will progressively reach lower year levels. Schools that are not already experimenting with in-person assessment design across Year 7-10 will be playing catch-up.

Sources: [New limit on take-home assessment tasks from Term 4 2026](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/news/all/new-take-home-assessment-rules) -- NESA / NSW Government, Term 4 2026; [Changes to ACE Rules concerning take-home assessment tasks and the use of artificial intelligence](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/news/all/changes-ace-rules-take-home-tasks-artifical-intelligence) -- NESA / NSW Government, September 2026; [NSW moves to protect AI academic integrity in HSC assessments](https://www.theeducatoronline.com/k12/news/nsw-moves-to-protect-ai-academic-integrity-in-hsc-assessments/289112) -- The Educator K-12, 2026.

### 4. Google expands Gemini study tools to younger students -- including those already in Australian classrooms

**Google / EdTech Magazine | 1-2 October 2026**

Google announced on 1-2 October that its Gemini student hub is now available to all Google Workspace for Education users, including those below 18, via updated parental controls and school administrator settings. The hub includes AI-powered study notebooks (adaptive, quiz-based learning from uploaded class materials), flashcard generation, practice quizzes and Gemini-powered contextual help tied to Classroom assignments -- where the teacher's instructions and curriculum materials shape the AI response.

The expansion means Gemini study tools are now active by default in school Workspace tenancies unless administrators have explicitly disabled them. Google has also launched new teacher-facing tools: automatic draft feedback on writing assignments and AI-generated rubric suggestions.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Most Australian independent schools run on Google Workspace for Education. This is not a tool students need to seek out: it is embedded in the platform they already use for assignments. The question every school administrator needs to answer this week is whether Gemini is currently active in their tenancy and for which year levels. This is a configuration decision, not a default a school implicitly accepted. It is also a curriculum opportunity: Google has designed the student hub to scaffold learning from a teacher's own materials rather than substitute for it, which is architecturally different from an open chatbot -- but that distinction needs to be taught, not assumed.

Sources: [Google Launches New AI Tools Purpose-Built for Education](https://edtechmagazine.com/k12/article/2026/10/google-launches-new-ai-tools-purpose-built-education) -- EdTech Magazine, October 2026; [Google Opens Gemini AI Study Tools to Younger Students](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/10/01/google-opens-classrooms-gemini-study-tools-to-younger-students-xcxwbn/) -- Winbuzzer, 1 October 2026; [Google Workspace Updates: Find your learning tools all in one place with the student hub in Gemini](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/find-your-learning-tools-all-in-one-place-with-the-student-hub-in-Gemini.html) -- Google Workspace Updates blog, October 2026.

### 5. Australian AI standards consultation closes Friday -- schools and sectors can still submit

**Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet / Office of AI | September 18 -- October 9, 2026**

The federal Office of AI's consultation on "Getting it right: Building AI infrastructure that works for Australia" closes on Friday 9 October. The paper proposes Australia's first nationally consistent AI standards, currently focused on mandatory conditions for large data centres and AI model training rather than obligations on deploying organisations. Education is not the primary focus, but the consultation explicitly includes questions about sectoral impacts, and it will inform AI regulation that will govern school-facing tools.

Separately, the federal Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence -- appointed by both chambers on 20 August -- has accepted submissions and is now working toward its November 30 reporting deadline. The committee's terms include the adequacy of Australia's education system for an AI-transformed workforce.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The submission window for shaping Australia's AI regulatory landscape is closing. An independent school that has spent three years navigating AI in its classrooms has lived evidence that policy makers will not have otherwise: what the national schools framework does and does not cover, where current guidance is insufficient, and what schools need from vendors to make sound procurement decisions. AISNSW and ISA are the most efficient channel, but coordinating a school-level contribution is worthwhile. The November committee report will be the most detailed federal picture of AI and education until at least the next parliamentary term.

Sources: [Getting it right: Building AI infrastructure that works for Australia](https://www.pmc.gov.au/resources/getting-it-right-building-ai-infrastructure-works-australia) -- Office of AI, PM&C, September 2026; [Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence](https://www.aph.gov.au/search/url/Inquiry/27445_24203_) -- Parliament of Australia, August 2026; [From patchwork to playbook: the Joint Select Committee on AI and what it means for business](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2026/august/from-patchwork-to-playbook-the-joint-select-committee-on-ai-and-what-it-means-for-business) -- Clayton Utz, August 2026.

## Worth Watching

**1. Stanford's K-12 AI evidence review: only 20 causal studies in 800+ papers**

Stanford's SCALE initiative published a comprehensive 2026 review of the AI in K-12 evidence base, examining more than 800 academic papers. The headline finding is stark: only 20 studies provide causal evidence of AI's effect on student or teacher outcomes, and there are no high-quality causal studies in K-12 US settings for students. Most research was conducted in constrained conditions (short one-off experiments), on populations above 18, in non-English-language settings, and with short-term outcome measures. The review documents positive effects on task completion in maths and writing when AI is present, but raises the question of whether task performance is the same as learning. This is the strongest methodological case yet for schools demanding evidence of learning impact -- not just task performance -- from any vendor claiming their tool improves outcomes.

Sources: [The Evidence Base on AI in K-12: A 2026 Review](https://scale.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Evidence%20Base%20on%20AI%20in%20K-12%20Report.pdf) -- Stanford SCALE Initiative, 2026; [Stanford's 2026 Review Finds Thin Evidence Behind Many AI Classroom Claims](https://www.neuralclass.uk/articles/stanfords-2026-review-finds-thin-evidence-behind-many-ai-classroom-claims) -- Neural Class, 2026.

**2. AWS and Code for Schools AI literacy program targeting one million Australian students**

Amazon Web Services and Code for Schools have announced a national AI literacy program designed for K-12 students across public, independent and Catholic schools, with a goal of reaching one million students over three years. The curriculum is age-appropriate, covers ethical and practical AI skills, and includes resources for parents and teachers. Rollout is planned from early 2026 school terms. The program has received coverage in education media but no independent evaluation exists yet. Worth tracking because: it is curriculum-aligned and free, it targets the teacher-confidence gap directly, and if it scales it will be the most widely used AI literacy program in Australian schools. Whether the ethical framing is robust or superficial will only be clear once materials are publicly available for review.

Sources: [AWS and Code for Schools partner to empower the next generation of Australia's AI builders](https://www.aboutamazon.com.au/news/aws/aws-and-code-for-schools-partner-to-empower-the-next-generation-of-australias-ai-builders) -- About Amazon Australia, 2026; [Nationwide AI literacy program to launch in 2026](https://educationmattersmag.com.au/nationwide-ai-literacy-program-to-launch-in-2026/) -- Education Matters Magazine, 2026; [AWS and Code for Schools launch AI literacy for one million](https://itbrief.com.au/story/aws-code-for-schools-launch-ai-literacy-for-one-million) -- IT Brief Australia, 2026.
