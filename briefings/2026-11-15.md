# AI in EdTech Weekly -- 15 November 2026

*A research briefing for the AI Integration Lead, Sydney independent K-12 school. Covering 8--15 November 2026.*

---

## Top Stories

### 1. Education ministers restrict generative AI in primary schools and secondary assessments, launch Digital Detox Taskforce

**Australian Government Department of Education / Jason Clare MP | 2 November 2026**

At the Education Ministers Meeting on 2 November, all federal, state and territory ministers agreed to restrict student use of generative AI in primary schools and in secondary student assessments, with states, territories and school sectors left to determine how they implement the restriction. Ministers also agreed to introduce, trial or consider limits on classroom screen use more broadly. A new Australian Schools Digital Detox Taskforce will collect evidence, survey parents and teachers on digital devices, and report to government by mid-2027. Digital devices and AI will remain a standing agenda item at EMMs until at least the end of 2027. In a related decision, ministers agreed to move the Year 5 NAPLAN writing test from online back to pen and paper from 2028; Year 7 and 9 writing remain undecided.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the most direct policy directive Australian education ministers have issued on generative AI, and it falls squarely on the side of restriction in primary schools. For a Sydney independent school with a primary AI programme, the ministerial agreement -- even though enforcement sits with sectors -- creates the expectation that your approach is auditable. It also signals that the evidence-based caution in AERO's October guidance is now government policy in spirit, not just research advice. The NAPLAN pen-and-paper reversal reinforces the same direction of travel: skills acquired without technology are back on the ministerial agenda.

### 2. NESA limits HSC take-home assessment tasks to curb AI-assisted work

**NSW Education Standards Authority | Term 4 2026**

NESA has amended its ACE Rules so that from Term 4 2026, each HSC course may include at most one take-home task in its school-based assessment program, worth no more than 15 per cent of the school-based assessment component. The same restriction applies to Preliminary courses from Term 1 2027. The change was made at the request of NSW Education Minister Prue Car. NESA guidance continues to advise that AI-detection software should be only one input in a teacher's professional review and not the sole evidence of academic misconduct. All students must complete the compulsory All My Own Work (AMOW) module covering AI use before being entered in Preliminary or HSC courses. The NESA Board will develop additional subject-specific advice and teacher guidance for the 2028 HSC cohort.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The 15 per cent cap on take-home tasks is a concrete, implementable rule that removes ambiguity for school assessment coordinators and subject teachers. It reflects what several schools have been doing informally, but now it has a regulatory basis and applies to every NSW secondary school. The key practical question it raises for a Year 5 teacher is whether the same logic -- that AI makes unmonitored tasks unreliable -- should inform primary assessment design now, before it becomes compulsory. The AMOW requirement is also worth revisiting: if your school's version was written before 2026, it likely needs updating to reflect current AI capabilities.

Sources: [Changes to ACE Rules concerning take-home assessment tasks and the use of artificial intelligence](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/news/all/changes-ace-rules-take-home-tasks-artifical-intelligence) -- NSW Education Standards Authority; [New rules for schools on AI in take-home HSC assessments](https://education.nsw.gov.au/news/latest-news/new-rules-for-schools-on-ai-in-take-home-hsc-assessments) -- NSW Department of Education.

### 3. Stanford review confirms AERO's position: AI tools help teachers more than students

**Stanford SCALE / Chalkbeat | 13 November 2026**

A Stanford SCALE review of more than 800 academic papers on AI in K-12 education -- released in November and gaining wide coverage this week -- found that only 20 studies produced sufficiently strong causal evidence to draw conclusions. The main findings: AI tools can improve student performance while they are available, but the gains often do not transfer once the tool is removed; students given access to general-purpose chatbots sometimes performed worse on unassisted assessments than peers who studied without AI, suggesting they were learning to use the tool rather than learning the content; tools that scaffold reasoning or provide hints showed more consistent positive results than those giving direct answers; and teacher-facing applications -- planning, feedback, communication -- showed clearer and more consistent benefits than student-facing ones. Long-term effects on learning remain unknown across all tool types.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the second major international evidence review in a month to reach the same conclusions as AERO, and it is the most methodologically rigorous to date. The finding that general-purpose chatbots may actively hurt learning on unassisted assessments matters for primary school practice, where chatbot use is often unstructured and self-directed. The teacher-facing finding is also worth foregrounding in staff professional learning: there is a credible evidence case for teachers using AI, and a much weaker one for students using it without scaffolding. The 20-of-800 figure is now the go-to citation for any governance or procurement conversation about evidence standards.

Sources: [AI research review finds limited benefits for students, some upsides for teachers](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2026/11/13/ai-research-review-shows-tools-help-teachers-more-than-students/) -- Chalkbeat, 13 November 2026; [The Evidence Base on AI in K-12: A November 2026 Review](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/evidence-base-review/evidence-base-ai-k12-november-review) -- Stanford SCALE, November 2026.

### 4. Anthropic signs binding AI safety and privacy agreement with US teachers' unions for Claude for Teachers

**Education Week / Campus Security Today | 10--12 November 2026**

The American Federation of Teachers and the United Federation of Teachers announced a legally binding AI Safety and Privacy Standard for Schools agreement with Anthropic on 10 November 2026. The deal covers Claude for Teachers, Anthropic's educator-facing product, and automatically applies to existing school district agreements that use it. Key terms include: student and educator data to be used only for authorised educational purposes; schools to control data retention and deletion; student data not to be sold; and teachers to remain responsible for educational decisions. EdWeek notes the deal mirrors language Microsoft adopted in September, though it does not cover student-facing products built on Claude by third parties. The unions are still negotiating with OpenAI.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Binding contractual commitments from AI vendors on data privacy are increasingly the minimum expectation, and this deal sets a benchmark that Australian schools can cite in their own procurement processes. The carve-out for third-party products built on Claude is worth noting: a school that buys a Claude-powered edtech tool from another vendor does not automatically inherit these protections, and the standard FERPA-equivalent question -- "is this the Anthropic deal, or is this something else?" -- should be asked of any reseller. For Australian independent schools, the more immediate reference is the Privacy Act and APP obligations, but the contractual model is transferable.

Sources: [Anthropic Agrees to Protections for Teachers Who Use Claude](https://www.edweek.org/technology/anthropic-agrees-to-protections-for-teachers-who-use-claude/2026/11) -- Education Week, November 2026; [Teachers Unions and Anthropic Sign Binding School AI Agreement](https://campussecuritytoday.com/articles/2026/11/12/teachers-unions-and-anthropic-sign-binding-school-ai-agreement.aspx) -- Campus Security Today, 12 November 2026.

### 5. Google ties Search access to AI acceptance, then partially walks back after school backlash

**Forbes / NBC News / CNN | 3 November 2026**

Google announced on 3 November that K-12 student accounts would have Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search switched off by default until a school administrator actively enabled them. Schools that chose to enable Search but not the Gemini products would face a constraint: in the initial rollout, opting out of AI features meant losing Search access entirely. After immediate backlash from school administrators and advocacy groups, Google said schools could enable Search alone, without turning on the Gemini products, though enabling Search in that mode still includes some AI-integrated features. Critics, including Fairplay's advocacy group, accused Google of using access to Search -- a tool schools depend on daily -- to pressure adoption of its AI products. New York City and Los Angeles both moved in October to restrict generative AI on district devices.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Australian schools using Google Workspace for Education should check their current admin console settings, since the defaults have changed and the access linkages between Search and Gemini products are non-obvious. More broadly, this is the starkest example yet of a major platform using infrastructure dependency -- if you want Search, you take some AI -- as a distribution mechanism, and it makes the case for schools having an explicit, documented position on each product within a suite, rather than accepting the defaults. For a school that wants to use Google tools without student-facing generative AI, the burden is now on the administrator to configure that explicitly.

Sources: [Google will block search access for schools that ban AI use](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/google-search-access-schools-ai-tools/) -- CBS News, November 2026; [Google to shut off search for schools that opt out of AI](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/google-shut-search-schools-opt-ai-rcna607280) -- NBC News, November 2026; [Google will let schools turn off AI, but they may also lose Search](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/03/tech/google-ai-search-policy) -- CNN, 3 November 2026; [Google Will Cut Off Search Engine Access To Schools Banning Generative AI](https://www.forbes.com/sites/maryroeloffs/2026/11/03/google-will-cut-off-search-engine-access-to-schools-banning-generative-ai/) -- Forbes, 3 November 2026; [Why is Google cutting off Search from AI-free schools?](https://opb.org/article/2026/11/13/to-use-google-search-schools-have-to-keep-ai-overviews) -- OPB, 13 November 2026.

## Worth Watching

**1. Joint Select Committee on AI final report, due 30 November 2026**

The committee reports in a fortnight. Its terms of reference explicitly include the adequacy of Australia's education system for an AI economy, and its October interim letter called for a national AI literacy programme. If the final report includes funded curriculum or professional learning recommendations, it becomes the architecture on which ACARA, AITSL and state authorities will be expected to build. The political sensitivity following the vetting controversy adds uncertainty about whether the report will be acted on promptly. Worth reading on the day it is tabled: [Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Joint/Artificial_Intelligence/ArtificialIntelligence).

**2. eSafety Safer Online Futures Symposium, Sydney, 23 November 2026**

eSafety is holding a one-day symposium in Sydney on 23 November. The agenda includes AI-enabled harms, regulatory responses to deepfakes, and a session titled "Talking to machines: Children's experiences with AI assistants and companions." Given Commissioner Inman Grant's October parliamentary testimony on AI deepfakes targeting schoolchildren, the symposium is likely to surface practical guidance for schools on image-based abuse policies and AI companion risk. Watch the eSafety newsroom for materials released on the day: [Safer Online Futures: eSafety Symposium 2026](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/whats-on/online-safety-symposium).
