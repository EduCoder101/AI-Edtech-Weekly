# AI in EdTech Weekly -- 22 November 2026

## Top Stories

### 1. NESA rewrites take-home assessment rules to close the AI integrity gap

**NESA (NSW) | November 2026**

NESA has amended the ACE Rules (reference NESA 34/26) to cap take-home assessment tasks for Preliminary and HSC courses: schools may now include no more than one take-home task, worth at most 15% of the overall mark. The change applies to Year 12 students starting HSC courses from Term 4, 2026, and to Year 11 students beginning Preliminary courses in Term 1, 2027. The rule follows an August 2026 direction from the NSW Education Minister asking NESA to consider a moratorium on unsupervised take-home tasks while a broader review runs. NESA separately reiterated that schools should not rely on AI detection tools as a primary safeguard against malpractice.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the most operationally significant AI-and-assessment change Australian schools have seen this year. If your school includes Year 11 or 12 students, Term 1 2027 planning needs to account for the new cap. The explicit "do not rely on AI detectors" guidance also signals that NESA expects schools to redesign tasks, not simply police submissions. That is a substantial professional learning and curriculum design challenge for senior school faculties, and one that primary schools may face on a compressed timeline as the ministers' Digital Detox framework cascades down.

**Source:** [NESA -- Changes to ACE Rules concerning take-home assessment tasks and AI](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/news/all/changes-ace-rules-take-home-tasks-artifical-intelligence) | [NSW Government review of AI impact on student learning, Education Matters Magazine](https://educationmattersmag.com.au/nsw-government-to-review-ai-impact-on-student-learning/)

### 2. Digital Detox: what the November 18 coverage tells schools about implementation

**Department of Education / newscop.com.au / CathNews | November 2026**

New coverage this week (including a newscop.com.au explainer dated 18 November 2026) is translating the 2 November ministerial communiqué into practical terms for schools. The key decisions are: generative AI is to be restricted for primary school students in classrooms; secondary students are to be restricted from using it in assessments; states, territories and sectors each determine timing and mechanism. An Australian Schools Digital Detox Taskforce will collect evidence and survey parents and teachers, with mid-2027 advice to government. Digital devices and AI use in schools will remain a standing agenda item at Education Ministers Meetings until at least end-2027. The federal Department of Education has published the full communiqué and a companion document titled "A Digital Detox."

**Why it matters for K-12:** The ministerial direction does not change anything overnight -- implementation is jurisdiction by jurisdiction -- but it establishes the direction. For independent schools, the Catholic sector's response is worth watching: CathNews reported on 5 November that "schools to ban generative AI in classrooms," language that may overstates the communiqué's binding force but signals where Catholic school systems are setting their default position. The mid-2027 taskforce report will likely trigger the next wave of formal changes. Communicating clearly with parents now about your school's current position -- and what may change -- will pre-empt confusion.

**Sources:** [5 November Communiqué -- education.gov.au](https://www.education.gov.au/download/20190/communiques-education-ministers-meetings-2026/46401/education-ministers-meeting-communique-november-2026/pdf) | [A Digital Detox document -- education.gov.au](https://www.education.gov.au/download/20853/digital-detox/46397/document/pdf) | [Digital Detox: 3 Crucial Changes for Australian Schools -- newscop.com.au, 18 November 2026](https://newscop.com.au/2026/11/18/digital-detox-australian-schools-screen/) | [Schools to ban generative AI in classrooms -- CathNews, 5 November 2026](https://cathnews.com/2026/11/05/schools-to-ban-generative-ai-in-classrooms/)

### 3. Stanford review: only 20 rigorous AI studies found, none in US K-12 schools -- and engagement is not learning

**Stanford SCALE / GovTech / EdTech Innovation Hub | November 2026**

A November 2026 review from Stanford's SCALE centre surveyed more than 800 papers on AI in education and found only 20 that rigorously measured whether AI changed student or educator outcomes. None of the student-facing studies took place in US K-12 schools. The key finding: students using AI often perform better *during* tool use, but those gains frequently do not carry over to unassisted work. Students who practised with general-purpose AI chatbots performed worse on unassisted exams than peers who prepared with traditional methods. Tools that prompt reasoning rather than deliver answers showed better results. A prior systematic review of 28 K-12 intelligent tutoring studies (4,597 students) found generally positive effects, though smaller than expected.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the most useful piece of international evidence to put in front of anyone who points to vendor-supplied "research" to justify adopting a student-facing AI tool. The finding that engagement does not equal learning is directly relevant to primary teachers and to any school evaluating adaptive or tutoring products. The Stanford review also supports AERO's October guidance: Australia's evidence is thin for the same reason the global evidence is thin -- almost no one is measuring learning, and almost no studies are set in primary schools.

**Sources:** [The Evidence Base on AI in K-12: A November 2026 Review -- Stanford SCALE](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/evidence-base-review/evidence-base-ai-k12-november-review) | [Stanford Researchers Find Thin Evidence Behind AI Classroom Tools -- GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/stanford-researchers-find-thin-evidence-behind-ai-classroom-tools) | [Stanford report questions AI impact in K-12 -- EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/stanford-report-finds-limited-evidence-behind-ai-impact-in-k-12-classrooms)

### 4. Teachers say AI professional learning misses the mark -- they want sustained, practical guidance

**EdWeek | November 2026**

A November 2026 Education Week article, drawing on district instructional coaches and AI training leads, reports that most teachers find one-off AI professional development sessions inadequate. What teachers say they actually need: sustained learning over multiple sessions; practical guidance on identifying and responding to AI-assisted student work; and help thinking through how to redesign tasks rather than police submissions. A 2026 Springer study of 532 K-12 teachers found that few had access to repeated training, suggesting continuous learning opportunities are still rare. EdWeek separately reports (November 2026) that 68% of education school faculty want ongoing or multi-session AI training, yet many education school programmes make it optional.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The gap described here maps directly to what most Australian school AI professional learning programs look like in late 2026 -- concentrated awareness sessions, with limited follow-through. Schools planning their 2027 professional learning calendar should build in distributed, discipline-specific sessions rather than single whole-school days. The finding that teachers want assessment redesign guidance, not just detection guidance, reinforces the same message coming from NESA: the response to AI in classrooms is fundamentally a curriculum and pedagogy question.

**Sources:** [Teachers Pinpoint What's Missing in Professional Development About AI -- EdWeek, November 2026](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/teachers-pinpoint-whats-missing-in-professional-development-about-ai/2026/11) | [AI Raises Big Questions for the Future of Teacher Education -- EdWeek, November 2026](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/ai-raises-big-questions-for-the-future-of-teacher-education/2026/11) | [Artificial intelligence in K-12 instruction: the role of teacher professional development -- Springer, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40561-026-00442-4)

## Worth Watching

**1. Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence -- final report due 30 November 2026**

The federal Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence is due to present its final report to parliament in eight days. Its terms of reference explicitly include the adequacy of Australia's education system for an AI economy and workforce and skills implications. An early advice letter from the chair and deputy chair to the Prime Minister (October 2026) listed AI literacy and the workforce as priority areas. The full report may recommend specific funding, curriculum changes, or regulatory action affecting schools. Watch the Parliament of Australia website from 30 November.

**2. ESA/AERO national quality standards for edtech AI tools -- pilot underway**

Education Services Australia (ESA) and AERO are piloting a Responsible AI Standard to evaluate AI-enabled digital products used in Australian schools. The standard is designed to check that tools are aligned with evidence-based teaching practices, educationally effective and safe. If this pilot matures into a formal vetting scheme, it could significantly change which AI tools schools can procure with confidence. This is early-stage, but it is the first sign of a systematic approach to quality assurance for AI edtech in Australia.

**Source:** [AERO -- AI in Schools Explainer for Policymakers (October 2026)](https://www.edresearch.edu.au/sites/default/files/2026-10/aero-ai-in-schools-considerations-for-policymakers-aa.pdf) | [Schools get new guidance on AI in classrooms -- The Educator](https://www.theeducatoronline.com/k12/news/schools-get-new-guidance-on-the-use-of-ai-in-classrooms/289272)
