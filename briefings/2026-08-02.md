# AI in EdTech Weekly -- 2 August 2026

*A research briefing for the AI Integration Lead, Sydney independent K-12 school. Covering 27 July -- 2 August 2026.*

---

## Top Stories

### 1. eSafety tells schools their own photo streams are now the attack surface

**eSafety Commissioner / Human Rights Watch | 28-30 July 2026**

The eSafety Commissioner issued a new Online Safety Advisory on 28 July, "Sharing school imagery in the age of AI", after receiving more than 100 reports between January and March 2026 about anonymous accounts targeting schools, students and staff. The imagery behind the abuse was harvested almost entirely from school websites and official social media accounts, then turned into AI face swaps, fabricated stories about principals and teachers, memes, and sexualised deepfakes shared on TikTok and Instagram; eSafety also reports a significant number of recent cases of AI-generated child sexual exploitation material in school settings. The advisory recommends a risk-based approach to how schools collect, approve, post and store images, clearer consent processes, tighter privacy settings, and a complaints and incident plan. Two days later, Human Rights Watch publicly backed the advisory, connecting it to its 2024 finding that photos of Australian children had been scraped into AI training datasets, and called for stronger data-privacy protections for children. Sector coverage in EducationHQ and Education Matters carried the practical checklists to staff-room level.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Independent schools publish more identifiable student imagery than almost anyone: marketing, socials, newsletters, event galleries. This advisory reframes that practice as a safeguarding exposure, not a communications choice, and it lands with primary schools squarely in scope. The practical move is an audit of the school's public photo footprint and consent language before the next marketing cycle, plus an agreed incident plan for deepfake abuse of students or staff. Expect parents to ask about this; the advisory gives the school defensible language either way.

Sources: [School photo posts vulnerable to emerging AI threat](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/school-photo-posts-vulnerable-to-emerging-ai-threat) -- eSafety Commissioner, 28 July 2026; [Sharing school imagery in the age of AI](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/blogs/sharing-school-imagery-in-the-age-of-ai-risks-deepfakes-and-how-to-protect-students-and-staff) -- eSafety Commissioner, 28 July 2026; [Australia: School Photos Scraped for AI-Fueled Image Misuse](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/07/30/australia-school-photos-scraped-for-ai-fueled-image-misuse) -- Human Rights Watch, 30 July 2026; [Schools under attack: spike in AI-generated face swaps, memes and fabricated stories](https://educationhq.com/news/schools-under-attack-spike-in-ai-generated-face-swaps-memes-and-fabricated-stories-213018/) -- EducationHQ, late July 2026.

### 2. The first classroom RCT of a teacher-facing AI assistant: teachers saved time, students paid for it

**The Hechinger Report / Wharton working paper | syndicated 28-30 July 2026**

Coverage spread widely this week of one of the first randomised controlled trials of AI in real classrooms. In the working paper "Generative AI Can Harm Teaching" (Alp Sungu of Wharton with University of Pennsylvania colleagues including Angela Duckworth), 193 teachers of more than 2,800 middle and high school students in a Turkish private school chain were given a ChatGPT-based teaching assistant aligned to the national curriculum for ten weeks. Teachers saved preparation time, but students rated their classes as less enjoyable, less interesting and less important, and among previously weaker teachers, student achievement on externally administered exams fell, as did student confidence. The paper, released online in June and not yet peer-reviewed, reached mainstream audiences this week through Jill Barshay's Proof Points column.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the sharpest evidence yet against the comfortable assumption that teacher-facing AI is the safe end of the pool. The mechanism matters for professional learning design: harm concentrated where teachers took AI output into class without adapting it. Time saved is not benefit banked; the case this makes is for pairing every teacher-facing tool with training in curating and localising AI-generated material, and for watching student experience data, not just teacher satisfaction, when evaluating AI pilots.

Sources: [Teachers save time with AI. Their students may pay the price](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-in-teaching/) -- The Hechinger Report (Proof Points), syndicated 28-30 July 2026; [syndicated edition](https://ktvz.com/stacker-news/2026/07/28/teachers-save-time-with-ai-their-students-may-pay-the-price/) -- KTVZ/Stacker, 28 July 2026.

### 3. Two curriculum heavyweights: schools need a way to measure AI progress, not more enthusiasm

**Education Matters | c. 30 July 2026**

Paula Christophersen and Anna Kinnane, co-authors of the Australian Curriculum: Digital Technologies, argue in Education Matters that neither enthusiasm nor caution is a strategy. AI is already shaping how students research, write and think whether or not a school has a policy, and the gap they see in Australian schools is not willingness but measurement: schools cannot say where they actually stand. They propose treating AI as a whole-of-school disruption and adopting a structured maturity or self-assessment approach that identifies gaps across teaching, assessment, governance and capability, then turns ambition into a sequenced plan.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the most directly useful Australian think-piece of the week for a school leadership audience. It supplies the vocabulary for the conversation most schools are avoiding: an honest audit of current state before the next tool purchase. For a school already running AI professional learning, a maturity self-assessment is a cheap next step that also produces exactly the evidence a principal or board asks for, and it positions the school well for whatever reporting expectations flow from the coming national AI standards.

Sources: [Schools don't need more AI enthusiasm. They need a way to measure progress](https://educationmattersmag.com.au/schools-dont-need-more-ai-enthusiasm-they-need-a-way-to-measure-progress) -- Education Matters (Christophersen and Kinnane), c. 30 July 2026.

### 4. Turnitin data: more than half of Australian university submissions contain AI writing, and what that means downstream

**The Conversation | c. 28 July 2026**

A widely republished Conversation analysis examined Turnitin data showing 53.6% of Australian tertiary submissions between October 2025 and April 2026 contained AI-assisted writing, with about 10% more than 80% AI-written. The comparable heavy-use figure for Australian secondary students is far lower, around 5-6% of submissions. The authors' central caution: detection scores indicate the likelihood of AI-assisted writing, not misconduct, and universities should respond with education-specific AI tools and assessment redesign rather than a detection arms race. Australia, they note, leads the world in per-capita use of Claude, with coursework a major share.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Today's Year 5 students will sit every high-stakes assessment of their lives in this environment. The detection-is-not-a-verdict caution applies with more force in schools, where the evidence base for detector reliability on younger students' writing is even thinner; a school tempted to run detector tools on student work should read this first. The secondary-school figures also give a rare calibrated baseline for how much fully outsourced AI writing is actually occurring below Year 12.

Sources: [More than 50% of Australian university assignments used AI. How should unis respond?](https://theconversation.com/more-than-50-of-australian-university-assignments-used-ai-how-should-unis-respond-287179) -- The Conversation, c. 28 July 2026.

### 5. The EU's AI transparency rules take effect: chatbots must say they are AI, deepfakes must be labelled

**European Commission / EU AI Act | 2 August 2026**

The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations came into force across the EU on 2 August. Chatbots must disclose that they are AI, synthetic content must carry machine-readable marking, and deepfakes and AI-generated text on matters of public interest must be labelled, with fines up to EUR 15 million or 3% of global turnover. Separately, the June "Digital Omnibus" deferred the Act's high-risk obligations for education AI, which cover systems used for admissions, assessment and progress monitoring, from August 2026 to December 2027.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The world's most-watched AI regime has just made AI disclosure and deepfake labelling a legal norm, the same fortnight Australia's own standards head to National Cabinet. Two signals for schools: disclosure-by-default is where policy is heading, which is worth mirroring now in academic integrity rules (students declare AI use; the school labels AI-generated content in its own communications); and the education high-risk category, though deferred, remains the emerging template for how "AI in assessment" will eventually be governed, including for vendors selling into Australia.

Sources: [Guidelines on transparency obligations for providers and deployers of AI systems](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems) -- European Commission, July 2026; [EU AI Act: what actually applies on 2 August 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/07/17/eu-ai-act-what-actually-applies-on-2-august-2026/) -- Technology.org, 17 July 2026.

## Worth Watching

**1. National Cabinet's August meeting on national AI standards, now imminent**

Carried over for a third week: the draft framework of legislated Australian AI standards, explicitly covering education and training, goes to National Cabinet this month, with legislation intended for early 2027. No date or new detail emerged this week. The eSafety advisory and HRW's privacy call both feed the case for children-specific provisions, so the education content of the standards may be firmer than the 15 July announcement suggested. Expect the outcome to land with little warning.

Source: [AI in Australia's Interests](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/ai-australias-interests) -- Prime Minister of Australia, 15 July 2026.

**2. Salamanca's "Sally": the first humanoid AI teaching assistant paused over data privacy**

A New York district's plan to put Sally, a lifelike humanoid AI teaching assistant, into classrooms this autumn was put on hold after a New York Focus report (28 July) triggered debate and the district moved to negotiate enhanced student data privacy agreements with the state. Flagged because embodied classroom AI is coming to the vendor market regardless, and because the pause shows data-privacy agreements, not pedagogy, are where these proposals will be won or lost. An Australian Conversation piece this week argued the idea is less strange than the headlines suggest.

Sources: [Meet Sally, the AI Teaching Assistant a NY School Put on Hold](https://nysfocus.com/2026/07/28/salamanca-ai-teaching-assistant-interview) -- New York Focus, 28 July 2026; [A lifelike robot called 'Sally' was going to teach in a US school. It's not as weird as it sounds](https://theconversation.com/a-lifelike-robot-called-sally-was-going-to-teach-in-a-us-school-its-not-as-weird-as-it-sounds-288493) -- The Conversation, late July 2026.
