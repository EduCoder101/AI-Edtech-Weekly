Weekly briefing · 24 August – 30 August 2026

National Cabinet locks in AI legislation for 2027 as every education department converges on supervised assessment, and the school photo question turns from advice into action

Issue of 30 August 2026

At a glance

The national AI regulatory picture firmed this week: National Cabinet on 26 August backed the Commonwealth's plan to legislate national AI laws and mandatory data centre standards from early 2027, while an ABC national scan showed every education department moving assessment back under supervision, with no system betting on AI detection. In the ACT, the eSafety Commissioner's school imagery advisory started changing visible practice, with at least one Canberra school stripping every photo from its website. Internationally, OpenAI expanded free ChatGPT for Teachers past 300,000 US educators under a first-of-its-kind 16-state privacy agreement, and a Bocconi randomised trial found ChatGPT lifts the quality of student work but not its originality, while critical-thinking training does the reverse. The through-line is that governance is hardening while the vendor land-grab for teachers accelerates.

Key signals

  • National Cabinet on 26 August affirmed the Commonwealth's plan to legislate national AI laws and mandatory standards for large data centres across all nine governments, with legislation intended in early 2027.
  • ABC's 25 August national scan found every state education department is tightening AI cheating responses, led by NSW's proposed moratorium on unsupervised take-home assessment now awaiting NESA advice due before Term 4 begins on 12 October.
  • A Canberra Times feature on 29 August reported at least one Canberra Catholic school has removed all photographs from its website in response to eSafety's school imagery advisory, leaving a single image of an empty playground.
  • OpenAI announced on 26 August that 55 more US school systems are adopting free ChatGPT for Teachers, passing 100 K-12 organisations and 300,000 educators, under a 16-state National Data Privacy Agreement it calls a first for the industry.
  • A randomised trial of more than 1,000 Bocconi undergraduates found ChatGPT access lifted work quality by nearly a full rubric point but did not increase originality, while critical-thinking training produced unique ideas without improving scores; only students with both showed both effects.

Considerations for the Executive

  • Adjust assessment schedules on the assumption that supervised demonstration of learning becomes the default this term: the ABC scan gives the executive a documented national trend to cite, and NESA's advice in September will set the benchmark independent schools are measured against.
  • Take a deliberate, minuted position on student imagery before parents raise the empty playground story: decide what goes on the open web versus authenticated portals, confirm consent settings cover AI misuse, and agree the response plan for a deepfake report.
  • Treat every free vendor offer reaching staff, including Microsoft Elevate and any ChatGPT for Teachers equivalent, as a procurement decision with an exit cost: the useful precedent to raise with ISA or AISNSW is OpenAI's sector-wide privacy agreement, negotiated once for many schools rather than school by school.

1

National Cabinet backs Commonwealth AI legislation, with laws due in early 2027

Prime Minister of Australia / National Cabinet | 26 August 2026

The outcome flagged in last week's Worth Watching has landed. On 26 August, National Cabinet affirmed the Commonwealth's plan to legislate national artificial intelligence laws and mandatory standards for large data centres, converting the July announcement of Australian Standards for AI into a shared commitment across all nine governments. Legal analysis published in the days after the meeting (Clayton Utz, Baker McKenzie) confirms the Commonwealth intends to legislate in early 2027, with the standards covering large-scale AI compute facilities, energy, water and community impacts, and conditions associated with delivering AI training.

Sources: Meeting of National Cabinet – Prime Minister of Australia, 26 August 2026; Nine governments, one rulebook: National Cabinet backs mandatory AI and data centre standards – Clayton Utz, August 2026; Australia Charts a New Course on AI Regulation and Policy – Baker McKenzie, August 2026; Australia Sets New AI Data Centre Rules After National Cabinet – The ABJ, 27 August 2026.

Why it mattersThe immediate provisions are about infrastructure, not classrooms, but two things matter for schools. First, Australia now has a settled national trajectory: one rulebook, legislated, from early 2027, which means the era of purely voluntary AI frameworks is closing and school-sector obligations are more likely to arrive as regulation than guidance. Second, nothing school-specific emerged from National Cabinet, so the live education policy action remains at state level (NESA's assessment review, SA's royal commission). Worth a one-line update to the principal: the national legislative timetable is now real, and the school's AI governance documentation should be maintained with early 2027 in view.
2

ABC maps how every Australian education department is cracking down on AI cheating

ABC News | 25 August 2026

ABC News published a national scan of how education departments across the country are responding to students using AI to complete assessment work. The picture is convergent but uneven: NSW has moved hardest, with the proposed moratorium on unsupervised take-home assessment for senior students pending NESA's advice, while other state departments have adopted their own combinations of supervised assessment, revised task design and updated misconduct procedures. The piece sits alongside the government AI platforms already built for public school systems in NSW, Queensland and South Australia, and notes early research warning of risks to concentration, memory and deep reasoning where AI use is unstructured.

Sources: How education departments across the country are cracking down on students using AI to cheat – ABC News, 25 August 2026; Matter of urgency: NSW assessment overhaul aiming to curb AI in schools – EducationHQ, August 2026; Use of Artificial Intelligence by students – NESA.

Why it mattersThis is the clearest single reference yet for answering "what is everyone else doing?", a question boards and executive teams ask constantly. The direction across all systems is the same: assessment that counts is moving back under supervision, and AI-detection tools are not part of any department's answer. An independent school that has not yet adjusted its own assessment schedule now has a documented national trend to reference, not just one state's announcement. It is also a prompt to check that the school's academic integrity procedures describe a process (conversation, re-demonstration of learning under supervision) rather than relying on detection.
3

The empty playground: ACT schools begin stripping photos from websites over AI deepfake risk

Canberra Times / ACM network | 29 August 2026

A Canberra Times feature published this week reports that ACT schools are now weighing whether to stop publishing photos of students altogether, following the eSafety Commissioner's recent advisory on school imagery. At least one Canberra Catholic school has removed every photograph from its website, leaving a single image of an empty playground. Catholic Education Canberra-Goulburn's executive director said the system is "carefully considering how best to balance the benefits of sharing school life with our obligations to protect children and young people." The eSafety guidance notes that AI tools can now place a face into a video, fabricate scenes or generate sexually explicit material from an ordinary photograph, that reports from under-18s about digitally altered intimate images doubled last year, and that more than 100 reports of anonymous accounts targeting schools arrived between January and March 2026.

Sources: The empty playground: Inside the ACT school debate over AI and photo bans – Canberra Times, 29 August 2026; School photo posts vulnerable to emerging AI threat – eSafety Commissioner; Sharing school imagery in the age of AI – eSafety Commissioner.

Why it mattersLast week this was an advisory; this week it is schools visibly changing practice, and the story frames the decision every school now faces. Because the eSafety recommendations are guidelines rather than directives, schools are landing in very different places, from full photo removal to no change. The empty playground image is the cautionary tale for both directions: strip everything and the school's public face disappears; change nothing and the school owns the risk the regulator has now documented. The defensible middle position is a deliberate, minuted decision covering consent settings, what imagery goes where (open web versus authenticated parent portals), and a response plan for misuse. Expect parents to raise this, possibly with this article in hand.

OpenAI expands free ChatGPT for Teachers and signs a first-of-its-kind 16-state privacy agreement

OpenAI | 26 August 2026

OpenAI announced on 26 August that 55 additional US school systems across 20 states are adopting ChatGPT for Teachers, taking the programme past 100 K-12 organisations in 30 states and more than 300,000 educators with free access and training, now extended through June 2028. The more consequential element is the accompanying 16-state National Data Privacy Agreement, struck through the Student Data Privacy Consortium framework: a single privacy agreement districts can rely on instead of negotiating individually with OpenAI, which the company describes as a first for the industry. Commentary was pointed: The Street's analysis argued the giveaway "isn't about teachers, it's about 2028", positioning free access as market capture ahead of paid renewals, and eMarketer placed it within a deliberate three-way race with Google and Microsoft to become the default classroom AI provider.

Sources: Bringing ChatGPT for Teachers to more U.S. school districts – OpenAI, 26 August 2026; OpenAI expands free ChatGPT for Teachers to 55 school systems across 20 states – 9to5Mac, 26 August 2026; OpenAI's back-to-school giveaway isn't about teachers. It's about 2028. – The Street, 27 August 2026; Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic bet on classrooms for future AI sales – eMarketer, August 2026.

Why it mattersTwo signals travel to Australia. First, the privacy mechanism matters more than the free access: a standardised, sector-negotiated data agreement is exactly the instrument Australian sector bodies (ISA, AISNSW, NCEC) could strike with vendors, and worth raising in sector forums rather than each school negotiating alone. Second, the strategic analysis is the useful lens for every free offer now reaching Australian staffrooms, including Microsoft's Elevate for Educators and Google's teacher training push: free tools build habit and lock-in before procurement decisions are ever formally made. The evaluation question is not "is it free?" but "what does exit cost in three years?"
5

Bocconi randomised trial: ChatGPT lifts the quality of student work but not its originality

OpenAI and Bocconi University | published this week

A randomised controlled experiment with more than 1,000 first-year undergraduates at Bocconi University, released this week by OpenAI, tested four conditions on a real business case task: ChatGPT (GPT-4o) access, training in causal reasoning, both, or neither. Students with ChatGPT scored nearly a full point higher on a five-point rubric, with clearer logic and submissions resembling expert recommendations, but their ideas were not more original. Students given the critical-thinking training scored no better on the rubric but generated ideas their peers had not thought of. Only the group with both showed both effects. The pattern echoes earlier findings that AI assistance homogenises output: better average work, narrower range of thinking.

Sources: Better answers, broader thinking: What students gain from ChatGPT and critical-thinking training – OpenAI, August 2026; Bocconi trial: ChatGPT raised marks, not originality – ResultSense, 28 August 2026; OpenAI Study: ChatGPT and Critical Thinking Train Students Better Together – The University Network, August 2026.

Why it mattersRead alongside the OECD crutch effect, this is the most useful research framing of the year for professional learning: AI access and thinking instruction are complements, not substitutes, and neither alone produces what schools actually want. For a primary and secondary context the translation is direct: teaching students (and staff) to reason has measurable value that AI access does not replace, and AI use without explicit thinking instruction produces polished sameness. Caveats worth stating when citing it: OpenAI co-ran the study, it measured one task's immediate output rather than durable learning, and participants were undergraduates. It strengthens rather than settles the case.

Where experts disagree

Live debates worth understanding before deciding

Does AI improve student work?

One viewBocconi RCT: ChatGPT access lifted work quality by nearly a full rubric point, with clearer logic and near-expert output
The other viewOECD crutch effect: generic chatbot use improved practice performance but cut closed-book exam scores by 17 per cent
The tensionBoth are right about different things: AI reliably improves the artefact produced, while the evidence on what the student retains still runs the other way; schools must decide which outcome they are assessing

What is free teacher AI for?

One viewOpenAI: free access and a common privacy framework make responsible adoption simpler and reduce teacher workload
The other viewThe Street: the giveaway is market capture, building dependence ahead of 2028 renewals when free ends
The tensionBoth can be true simultaneously; the school's task is to take the workload benefit while pricing in the exit cost, not to pick a side

How far should schools go on student imagery?

One viewAt least one Canberra school has removed all photos from its website, treating publication itself as the risk
The other vieweSafety's recommendations remain guidelines, not directives, and most schools continue publishing with consent and privacy controls
The tensionFull removal trades away community connection and prospective-family visibility for risk elimination; the regulator has deliberately left the judgement with schools, so each needs its own reasoned position

Worth watching

Developments to keep an eye on

NESA's advice on the assessment moratorium is now due within weeks

The Deputy Premier asked for NESA's advice in time for schools to have clarity before Term 4, which begins Monday 12 October in NSW. That puts the Board's advice, and any differentiation by key learning area or exemptions for major projects, on track to land in September. Whatever NESA decides will shape independent school assessment practice well beyond the government system, and AISNSW guidance is likely to follow quickly. This is the single most consequential pending decision for NSW schools this term.

Sources: Minns Government examines measures to halt AI's impact on cognitive development – NSW Government; HSC class of '27 on notice: NESA told to consider impact of AI in schools – The Mandarin, August 2026.

SA Royal Commission terms of reference due mid-September, hearings from 1 October

Still on schedule: the terms of reference for South Australia's Royal Commission into AI were promised within four to six weeks of the 10 August announcement, which points to mid-September, with commissioners commencing 1 October 2026 and a final report due 1 July 2027. Education, including schools, is confirmed as a core focus. Once the terms are published, the submission window is the moment for a practising school's classroom evidence to reach the record, directly or through ISA.

Sources: Royal Commission into Artificial Intelligence – Office for AI, SA Government; Royal Commission into Artificial Intelligence announced – SA Department of the Premier and Cabinet.
