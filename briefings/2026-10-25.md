# AI in EdTech Weekly -- 25 October 2026

*A research briefing for the AI Integration Lead, Sydney independent K-12 school. Covering 18 October -- 25 October 2026.*

## Top Stories

### 1. AERO tells schools to use AI sparingly, avoid it entirely in early primary

**Australian Education Research Organisation / ABC News / EducationHQ | 14 October 2026**

The Australian Education Research Organisation released its first dedicated AI in Schools resource package on 14 October, producing separate explainers for teachers and school leaders and for policymakers. The overall stance is cautious guardrails rather than a ban. AERO CEO Jenny Donovan said AI should be avoided entirely in the first three years of primary school, used carefully through the remainder of primary, and introduced more deliberately in secondary where students are closer to environments where AI is already expected. The guidance sets out five considerations for school leaders and teachers: prioritise evidence-based teaching, monitor for risks to learning, set clear expectations for AI use, rethink assessment and feedback, and apply a culturally responsive lens that respects First Nations knowledges. Teachers are told they may use AI to draft feedback, but must retain professional responsibility for judging student work and never let AI replace the thinking students need to do for themselves.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the most authoritative Australian guidance on AI in schools published to date, and it comes from the national evidence body. Two things stand out. First, AERO explicitly names early primary school as a risk zone -- an unusual move that gives school leaders a defensible position for limiting student-facing AI in Year 1-3 classrooms. Second, the guidance is grounded in a frank admission: AERO found that most existing research is short-term, conducted overseas, focused on engagement rather than learning, and rarely set in a primary context. That evidence gap is itself a policy-relevant finding. A school presenting its AI strategy to a board or principal should reference this guidance directly.

Sources: AERO, [AI in Schools resources](https://www.edresearch.edu.au), 14 October 2026; ABC News, ["Education research body urges schools to use AI sparingly due to learning risks"](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-10-14/ai-in-schools-warning-affecting-long-term-learning/107263444), 14 October 2026; EducationHQ, ["Little robust evidence to show AI's impact on student learning: AERO"](https://educationhq.com/news/little-robust-evidence-to-show-ais-impact-on-student-learning-aero-215921/), October 2026; Education Matters, ["New AERO guidance tackles AI use in Australian schools"](https://educationmattersmag.com.au/new-aero-guidance-tackles-ai-use-in-australian-schools/), October 2026; The Educator K-12, ["Schools get new guidance on the use of AI in classrooms"](https://www.theeducatoronline.com/k12/news/schools-get-new-guidance-on-the-use-of-ai-in-classrooms/289272), October 2026.

### 2. NESA caps take-home assessment at 15 per cent and adds AI rules for HSC courses, effective Term 4 2026

**NSW Education Standards Authority / Independent Schools NSW | October 2026**

NESA's updated ACE Rules take effect for Year 12 students beginning HSC courses from Term 4 2026, with Preliminary course changes following in Term 1 2027. Schools are now limited to one take-home assessment task worth no more than 15 per cent of the school-based assessment component. Courses with major works or HSC practical exams are exempt. On AI specifically, NESA's notice states that marks must not reflect a student's use of generative AI, and that schools should not rely on AI detection tools as the main safeguard against malpractice. Schools are required to maintain policies on whether generative AI is permissible in any given assessment.

**Why it matters for K-12:** NSW independent schools need to audit their current Year 12 assessment schedules now. The cap may require restructuring tasks that exceed the 15 per cent threshold. The explicit prohibition on AI detection tools as a primary safeguard reflects growing recognition across the sector that detection is unreliable, and moves responsibility back to task design -- which is where it belongs. Schools that have not yet built AI-use policies into their assessment frameworks have a compliance deadline, not just a best-practice horizon.

Sources: NESA, ["New limit on take-home assessment tasks from Term 4 2026"](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/news/all/new-take-home-assessment-rules), 2026; NESA, ["New rules for schools on AI in take-home HSC assessments"](https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/new-rules-ai-education), 2026; Independent Schools NSW, ["Changes to HSC school-based assessment"](https://www.isnsw.edu.au/newsroom/changes-to-hsc-school-based-assessment), 2026; The Educator K-12, ["NSW moves to protect AI academic integrity in HSC assessments"](https://www.theeducatoronline.com/k12/news/nsw-moves-to-protect-ai-academic-integrity-in-hsc-assessments/289112), 2026.

### 3. Large longitudinal study finds AI raises homework scores but cuts exam performance -- damage takes two years to surface

**CEPR Discussion Paper / Psychology Today | June--October 2026**

A CEPR discussion paper (DP21577) by Strömberg, Lei and Wu tracked 26,811 Chinese secondary school students over 30 months after AI homework-assistance tools were introduced. The headline finding: AI use raised homework scores but was associated with weaker unaided exam performance -- secondary reporting cites declines of up to 24 per cent, with the full damage to high-stakes exams not becoming visible until nearly two years after adoption began. The paper coins the phrase "generative AI learning penalty." Students who maintained strong personal effort while using AI showed significantly less learning loss. An October 2026 randomised experiment on university students found a contrasting pattern: AI used as a tutor (not a writer) produced gains that partially persisted a week later. The authors call the distinction "augmentation versus automation."

**Why it matters for K-12:** This study is the most important piece of evidence to come out of 2026 for school leaders thinking about student-facing AI. It directly corroborates AERO's caution, and it provides a mechanistic explanation: when AI does the thinking, students stop practising the cognitive processes that build durable knowledge. The 30-month timeframe matters particularly for primary school: a Year 3 student who begins heavy AI use in 2026 may not show the downstream effects on unaided performance until Year 5 or Year 6. The finding that effort is the mediating variable -- not AI use per se -- has direct implications for task design and for how schools frame AI capability with students. Use AI to think harder, not to think less.

Sources: Strömberg, Lei and Wu, ["The Generative AI Learning Penalty: Evidence from Chinese Secondary Education"](https://cepr.org/), CEPR Discussion Paper DP21577, 2026; Psychology Today, ["A Study of 26,000 Students Shows the AI Learning Trap"](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-power-of-experience/202606/a-study-of-26000-students-shows-the-ai-learning-trap), June 2026; tryplox, ["AI Learning Penalty: 26,000-Student Study Shows Hidden 2-Year Cost"](https://www.tryplox.com/news/ai-learning-penalty-26000-student-study), 2026.

### 4. South Australia's AI Royal Commission commences, with education squarely in scope

**Royal Commission into Artificial Intelligence (SA) / ACS itnews | October 2026**

South Australia's Royal Commission into Artificial Intelligence formally commenced on 1 October 2026. Dr Iain Ross AO chairs the Commission, with terms of reference covering how SA's education system should adapt to AI alongside regulatory and public-service considerations. The Commission is expected to cost up to $3 million and must deliver its final report by 1 July 2027. At the federal level, a Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence -- including adequacy of the national education system in its terms of reference -- is also underway, with a reporting deadline of 30 November 2026.

**Why it matters for K-12:** South Australia is the first Australian jurisdiction to put AI and education under Royal Commission scrutiny. Royal Commissions carry weight beyond their geographic scope: the findings and any recommendations will inform national conversation at exactly the moment the Joint Parliamentary Committee is also reporting. Schools and sector bodies that have not yet considered how to engage with these processes -- or whose principals want visibility of what is coming -- should track both timelines. Independent school peak bodies including ISA and AISNSW are well placed to make submissions.

Sources: Royal Commission into Artificial Intelligence (SA), [royalcommissionai.sa.gov.au](https://www.royalcommissionai.sa.gov.au/), 2026; ACS itnews, ["South Australia announces AI royal commission"](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2026/south-australia-announces-ai-royal-commission.html), 2026; ACS itnews, ["South Australia names AI Royal Commission panel"](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2026/south-australia-names-ai-royal-commission-panel.html), 2026; ABC News, ["Why is South Australia holding a royal commission into AI and why is it necessary?"](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-11/why-is-south-australia-holding-royal-commission-into-ai/107018396), 11 August 2026.

### 5. OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026: strong adoption but thin evidence on what GenAI use in education actually works

**OECD | January 2026 (ongoing reference)**

The OECD's Digital Education Outlook 2026, released in January, examines emerging research on generative AI in education across member countries. Key findings reported in subsequent coverage: TALIS data shows that at least 50 per cent of AI-using teachers in several OECD countries use AI to mark schoolwork. Adoption is rapid, but the report flags the same evidence gap AERO later identified -- robust causal studies on learning outcomes remain rare. A separate OECD/European Commission AI Literacy Framework for Primary and Secondary Education was published in June 2026.

**Why it matters for K-12:** Australia is not an outlier: the OECD framing confirms that high adoption and thin evidence is a global pattern, not a local one. The AI literacy framework for primary and secondary is worth downloading as a cross-check against any home-grown school framework your school is developing. It provides a level of international legitimacy useful in governance conversations.

Sources: OECD, [Digital Education Outlook 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026_062a7394-en.html), January 2026; OECD, [Empowering Learners for the Age of AI](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/empowering-learners-for-the-age-of-ai_65cd27d4-en.html), June 2026.

## Worth Watching

**1. Federal Joint Select Committee on AI -- education terms of reference, reporting 30 November 2026**

The committee's terms of reference include the adequacy of Australia's education system to prepare students and teachers for AI. Its report lands five weeks from now. Unlike state commissions, the Joint Select Committee reports to the full federal parliament. What it recommends on curriculum, teacher training, or AI regulation in schools will shape funding and policy conversations well into 2027. Schools and sector bodies that want to understand what is coming -- and potentially make a submission -- have a narrow window.

**2. arXiv preprint: "Will It Teach as Intended? How Teachers Configure Educational AI Chatbots" (2026)**

A 2026 preprint examined how teachers actually configure AI chatbot tools for classroom use -- and whether the results match pedagogical intent. The study is preliminary and not yet peer-reviewed, but it addresses a question that rarely gets asked: between the design of an AI tool and the learning experience a student actually has, teacher configuration is a critical and largely invisible variable. If the findings hold up, they will complicate the assumption that deploying a "good" AI tool produces good outcomes without deliberate teacher intervention in setup and framing.
