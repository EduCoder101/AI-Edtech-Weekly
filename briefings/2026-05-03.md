## Top Stories

### 1. ISNSW engagED ICT Conference concludes -- AI strategy dominates the agenda

**Independent Schools NSW | 29 April -- 1 May 2026**

The Independent Schools NSW ICT Management and Leadership Conference, held this week at the Gold Coast Convention and Exhibition Centre under the theme "engagED -- Where innovation meets education," brought together ICT leaders from across the NSW independent school sector. Keynote speakers included Dr Sandra Peter and Dr Kai Riemer (University of Sydney Business School), both of whom work extensively on AI disruption and the future of work. The conference featured over 100 technology vendors and sessions on AI strategy, digital transformation, and school-level implementation. ISNSW offered scholarships to increase participation from a diverse range of member schools.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is the most significant gathering of independent school technology leaders in NSW, and its timing -- mid-semester, mid-year planning cycle -- means the conversations happening in those rooms will shape infrastructure decisions, platform choices, and PL priorities for the rest of 2026. The presence of Peter and Riemer as keynotes signals that ISNSW is framing AI as a leadership and strategic question, not purely a technology one. If your school's ICT leadership attended, it is worth debriefing on what commitments or conversations emerged.

Sources: Independent Schools NSW, [ISNSW ICT Management and Leadership Conference 2026](https://www.isnsw.edu.au/featured-events/ict-conference), 29 April -- 1 May 2026; Cambium Networks, [Independent Schools NSW | April 29 -- May 1, 2026](https://community.cambiumnetworks.com/t/independent-schools-nsw-april-29-may-1-2026-brisbane-australia/107807), 2026.

### 2. eSafety urges schools to report AI-generated deepfakes as cases double

**eSafety Commissioner | ongoing, updated 2026**

Australia's eSafety Commissioner has issued an urgent call for schools to report AI-generated deepfake incidents, after reports of digitally altered intimate images involving young people under 18 more than doubled in 18 months -- exceeding the total number received in the seven preceding years. eSafety has released an updated Toolkit for Schools with step-by-step guidance for managing deepfake incidents. Four out of five reports involve female targets. Following eSafety enforcement action against "nudify" services used in Australian schools, three platforms have been removed from the Australian market. In some states, creating or sharing AI-generated explicit content is now a criminal offence.

**Why it matters for K-12:** This is no longer a rare or emerging problem -- it is a pattern, and it is in Australian schools now. The doubling of reports in under two years suggests that awareness among students is not a deterrent. Independent schools need a specific protocol for deepfake incidents that is distinct from existing cyberbullying policies: the legal context is different, eSafety has specific reporting pathways, and victim support needs are different from general online harm. The updated toolkit is a practical starting point and is worth sharing with your wellbeing and student services teams.

Sources: eSafety Commissioner, ["eSafety urges schools to report deepfakes as numbers double"](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-urges-schools-to-report-deepfakes-as-numbers-double), 2026; eSafety Commissioner, ["Deepfake damage in schools"](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/blogs/deepfake-damage-in-schools-how-ai-generated-abuse-is-disrupting-students-families-and-school-communities), 2026; eSafety Commissioner, ["eSafety moves against nudify services"](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-moves-against-services-used-to-nudify-australian-school-children), 2026.

### 3. Primary teachers report the sharpest AI-content literacy gap -- new EdWeek survey

**Education Week Research Center | April 2026**

A new survey by the Education Week Research Center, published in late April, found that 61% of primary school teachers say their students struggle "a lot" to distinguish AI-generated content from non-AI content -- a significantly higher proportion than middle school (44%) or secondary teachers (38%). The survey was conducted across February and March 2026 and complements an April EdWeek analysis arguing that schools are playing "catch-up" on media literacy at the same time AI content is accelerating in volume and sophistication. At least half of US states have enacted media literacy laws since 2024; Australia has no equivalent national requirement.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The primary years gap is the most directly relevant finding here. If your Year 5 students are already interacting with AI tools through NSWEduChat, they are encountering AI-generated text and images as normal -- but this survey suggests they lack the critical lens to interrogate that content. AI literacy at the primary level cannot simply be a scaled-down version of secondary AI ethics conversations; it needs to build from concrete skills (how do I check whether this is AI-generated?) toward more abstract critical thinking. This is a curriculum design question, not just a values one.

Sources: Education Week, ["Schools Play Game of Media Literacy Catch-Up as AI Use Rises"](https://www.edweek.org/technology/schools-play-game-of-media-literacy-catch-up-as-ai-use-rises/2026/04), April 2026; Media Literacy Now, [New U.S. Media Literacy Report](https://medialiteracynow.org/new-u-s-media-literacy-report-finds-state-media-literacy-policy-accelerating-as-lawmakers-respond-to-phones-social-media-and-ai/), 2026.

### 4. New York City releases preliminary AI guidance for 1.1 million students

**NYC Department of Education | active through 8 May 2026**

The New York City Department of Education has published preliminary AI guidance for its 1.1 million-student school system and is accepting public feedback until 8 May 2026, with a comprehensive playbook planned for June. Under the guidance, teachers may use AI for brainstorming, drafting communications, and lesson planning. Hard limits include: AI may not be used to assign grades, make disciplinary decisions, or collect biometric and behavioural data without strict oversight. The guidance also addresses student use in classrooms, data privacy, and vendor procurement.

**Why it matters for K-12:** NYC's framework is significant not because Australian schools need to follow it, but because of the distinctions it draws. The explicit prohibition on AI grading and AI-driven discipline is a governance signal worth noting: these are exactly the uses that are most tempting under workload pressure and most risk-laden for students. Australian frameworks -- including the national AI in Schools Framework -- have been less explicit about hard limits. NYC's approach offers a template for schools wanting to move beyond aspirational principles toward enforceable policy.

Sources: NYC Department of Education, [Preliminary AI Guidance](https://www.schools.nyc.gov/about-us/news/morning-bell/contentdetails/morning-bell/2026/04/30/the-month-ahead-may-2026), April 2026; Chalkbeat, ["What NYC's new AI school rules say, and what still remains unclear"](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/03/24/preliminary-ai-policy-nyc-schools/), 2026; AI for Education, [AI + Education Weekly Update](https://www.aiforeducation.io/blog/ai-education-weekly-update-apr272026), 27 April 2026.

### 5. Students to co-write model AI policy for US schools -- a new governance model

**Education Week | April 2026**

AASA (The School Superintendents Association), Day of AI, and MIT RAISE have announced a July 2026 workshop in Boston at which one superintendent and two student representatives from each US state will work together to draft a model AI policy for K-12 schools. The initiative directly addresses what district leaders cite as a primary barrier to responsible AI adoption: the absence of clear, student-endorsed policy frameworks. Day of AI -- a free, curriculum-aligned AI literacy program from MIT -- is also active in Australia through Day of AI Australia, which has reached over 100,000 students and educators.

**Why it matters for K-12:** The idea of students co-designing the policy they will be governed by is both pedagogically sound and practically useful -- students often identify edge cases and values tensions that adults miss. The resulting policy will be shared publicly and is likely to circulate quickly in the Australian independent school context. Worth flagging this to your principal as a conversation-starter: should your school seek student input into its AI policy revision? Day of AI Australia's existing presence in Australian schools means the underlying curriculum infrastructure is already here.

Sources: Education Week, ["Students Will Take the Lead on Crafting a Model AI Policy for Schools"](https://www.edweek.org/technology/students-will-take-the-lead-on-crafting-a-model-ai-policy-for-schools/2026/04), April 2026; Day of AI Australia, [For Teachers](https://dayofaiaustralia.com/teachers/), 2026.

## Worth Watching

**1. LAK26 conference now concluded -- research outputs incoming**

The 16th Annual Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (Bergen, Norway, 27 April -- 1 May 2026) concluded this week. Flagged in last week's briefing as a high-quality venue for causal research on AI in classrooms, the conference presented the NC State "sticky teacher attention" paper among others. Proceedings and papers will be published over the next 4--6 weeks. Worth monitoring for findings relevant to AI tutoring system design and teacher circulation in primary classrooms.

**2. Character.AI's regulatory response to eSafety: a model for platform accountability?**

Following eSafety's transparency notice process, Character.AI introduced age assurance measures for Australian users in early 2026 and removed chat functionality from its under-18s experience. Chub.ai geo-blocked Australia entirely. This is the first significant instance of an AI companion platform materially changing its product for the Australian market in response to regulatory pressure -- and it happened without new legislation, using existing Online Safety Act powers. If this pattern holds, it suggests eSafety's transparency notice mechanism is more powerful than its formal enforcement profile suggests. Worth tracking as an emerging model for platform accountability in the Australian context.

Source: eSafety Commissioner, ["eSafety report shows AI companions are putting children at risk"](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-report-shows-ai-companions-are-putting-children-at-risk), March 2026.
